Design school promises clarity, while reality, shaped by real constraints, brings questions. Here, 13 designers and artists share what they had to unlearn about success, ambition, and creativity, reminding us that doubt is normal, detours are expected, and a meaningful  
practice takes time to build.

UNLEARNED

UNLEARNED

Christina Worner came to see that even within real-world constraints, design can still hold meaning and conceptual depth.

Shantell Martin reflects on trying to step away from art after graduation, only to realize that creativity is inseparable from who she is—and will always find its way back.

Freddie Öst discovered that love, not rebellion, is the most powerful and transformative force in creativity and life.

Jarrett Fuller learned that design is an evolving way of thinking, constantly reshaped by culture and technology—and that this uncertainty can be freeing.

Mario Carrillo shows how stepping away from a predefined tech career and embracing uncertainty led  
him to follow his curiosity  
into computational art.

Erik Kessels argues that the most meaningful work begins in the “backyard” of raw experimentation before being refined for the “front yard” of the professional world.

Stefan Sagmeister  
challenges the fear around entrepreneurship, arguing  
that running a design studio  
is relatively simple, low-risk,  
and ultimately liberating.

Sarah Boris shows  
how strategically reshaping  
a portfolio to reflect a studio’s identity helped her secure a desired role at the Barbican Centre in London.

Raissa Pardini presents unlearning as a working method, questioning Western design habits, adapting her sources of inspiration during the pandemic, and embracing reinvention.

Cherry-Ann Morgan found that embracing her cultural heritage can challenge Western design norms and open space for more diverse ways of creating.

Zipeng Zhu landed his first design job the day after graduation—at Sagmeister & Walsh—an experience that showed him life after school  
is demanding and ultimately well worth it.
