Very little has changed in the world of web performance since [last week](https://csswizardry.com/2026/08/web-perf-wednesday-003-native-spa-metrics-have-arrived/), at least not enough to justify turning routine ebbs and flows into urgent work. The [July 2026 CrUX release](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/crux/release-notes/) reported no significant changes, and there was no similarly consequential browser or measurement change worth drawing attention to. That makes for a useful pause: teams are better off spending time checking the evidence they already have, finishing the investigations that busy weeks can deprioritise, and making one well-understood improvement than by chasing novelty for its own sake.

## Need Help with a Performance Question?

A quieter news week doesn’t mean your own performance questions have gone quiet, too! If a dashboard moved without an obvious cause, a slow journey still refuses to explain itself, or a browser-specific problem is holding up a decision, I’m always happy to take a look.

You don’t need a fully formed brief. Just bring the odd trace, or a loose question, and we can work out what the evidence is actually saying. [Get in touch](https://csswizardry.com/contact/) and start a conversation.
