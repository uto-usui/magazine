Summary:  Well-written informational microcopy should be clear, concise, and have character.

[Informational microcopy](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/3-is-of-microcopy/) helps users understand an interface and complete their tasks. Without clear information, users struggle to make informed decisions and navigate UI effectively. Follow the 3 C's — clarity, concision, and character — to ensure your microcopy delivers what users need.

-   [Clarity](#toc-clarity-1)
-   [Concision](#toc-concision-2)
-   [Character](#toc-character-3)

## Clarity

When writing for clarity, make sure your copy **delivers the key information clearly and usefully**. Treat this goal as the primary task of all informative microcopy; since, without clarity, informative microcopy fails to achieve its purpose.

You can ensure your informative microcopy is clear by considering the following:

-   **[Signposting](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/navigation-you-are-here/),** so users know their location on the site
-   **[Information scent](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-scent/),** so users are informed where to go next
-   **Relevance,** so users know why something applies or is valuable to them
-   [**Appropriate terminology**](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/technical-jargon/)**,** so users are informed of what words and phrases mean

### Example: Mastercard

When advertising credit cards, Mastercard strives to be **relevant** and **use appropriate terminology** for its target audience. The following content appeared on Mastercard’s student-card comparison page.

![The Capital One Quicksilver Student Cash Rewards credit card with a product description that features copy like "Enjoy peace of mind with $0 Fraud Liability" "Lock your card" and "Build your credit with responsible card use."](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2025/11/25/studentcard_ZQIZqNI.png)

_Mastercard’s microcopy explained standard credit-card benefits, such as_ Fraud Liability_,_ Lock your cards, _and_  Build your credit with responsible card use _in an approachable and clear way to inform a student audience new to credit-card-industry terms and standards._

The credit-card industry is filled with jargon and industry standards that people new to credit (like students) may not understand. So, to better address the needs of this specific audience, Mastercard explains common card features like fraud liability, card locking, and emphasizes the card as an opportunity to build credit.

In contrast, the listing on Mastercard’s comparison page for low-interest cards featured more specifics and industry jargon like _APR_ and _rotating categories_ to target the needs of a more general, interest-focused audience.

![Capital One Quicksilver Cash Rewards Credit Card with a product description with copy that says "No rotating categories or sign-ups needed to earn cash rewards," and "0% intro APR on purchases and balance transfers for 15 months."](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2025/11/25/generalcard_uEBSTFz.png)

_When advertising a similar card, Mastercard featured interest-related specifics like_ 0% intro APR on purchases _rather than explaining industry standards._

## Concision

Concision **ensures that your microcopy is read** by busy users and that essential, clarifying information doesn’t get cut off by truncation. Out of the 3 C’s, concision should come second, meaning that a few extra words can be included if they increase the understandability of a phrase or sentence.

Still, concision should not be ignored. Microcopy is, by definition, [shorter than 3 sentences,](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-copy-sizes/) so every word must serve a purpose.

To achieve concision,

-   **Avoid [common errors](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/rewriting-content-brevity/) that** unnecessarily lengthen text
-   **Follow standard character lengths** for titles and descriptions, as set by search engines, to prevent confusing truncation or search-engine revisions
-   **Focus on the words essential to the message and** cut down as many of the rest as possible.

### Example: Owala

This microcopy from Owala is an excellent example of how to deliver multiple pieces of information concisely. When replacing the _Add to Cart_ button label with _Out of Stock_  - _Notify Me_ on its product pages, Owala leads with the most salient information (_Out of Stock)_, ensuring that users reading the bare minimum understand the barrier to their task. For users with the time to read a little bit further, Owala uses quickly scannable fragments to detail a second piece of essential information, providing users with a solution and getting them back to shopping faster. 

![Button with "Out of stock - notify me."](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2025/11/25/owala_AxKIQ0A.png)

_Owala’s button label concisely informs users of the problem_ and _the solution, reducing friction._

### Example: World Health Organization (WHO)

Concision boosts both scannability and SERP appearance. For example, notice how WHO’s page title _Vaccination Information Hub_ does not include the article “the” upfront. Adding this extra word would decrease scannability and also increase the title length. Page titles over 60 characters tend to be truncated when appearing in search results. 

![A page title that says "Vaccination Information Hub" above it's search engine result that also says "Vaccination Information Hub."](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2025/11/25/frame-28_tw1ih1x.png)

_WHO: The page title does not include unnecessary modifiers, making it easier for users to scan it and for search engines to render it untruncated._

There are standard character limits for other forms of microcopy, like summaries and descriptions (e.g., 160 characters). These often come from search engines, which prefer meta descriptions and titles that fit neatly in their results pages. Knowing these limits and writing within them in mind, like WHO did, helps prevent titles from getting cut off or rewritten on search-engine page results.

## Character

Character refers to tactics like tone and humor, or anything else that **humanizes microcopy and makes it memorable.**

Character should be the third priority for informative-microcopy writers, meaning it should be considered, but not get in the way of concision and clarity.

When crafting character, focus on your audience. Tailor tone, references, and strategies to their preferences; there’s no universal list of best practices. Start by choosing an appropriate tone of voice, then add other tactics if needed.

### Example: Ace Hardware

This header _Top Brands Under One Roof_, which appears on the Ace Hardware homepage, uses **a professional tone but a unique delivery** to describe the major brands Ace carries in a **memorable way**.

![Carousel titled "Top Brands Under One Roof" and in each card is a popular outdoors or tools brand like "Craftsman" "DeWalt" and "Yeti."](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2025/11/25/screenshot-2025-11-05-at-54900pm.png)

_Ace: The carousel heading_ The Top Brands Under One Roof _is professional and memorable._

As a hardware store chain, Ace appeals to its wide, yet action-oriented audience by maintaining a standard professional tone focused on no-frill information delivery. At the same time, the phrasing _under one roof_ makes this header stand out without compromising clarity.

### Example: OpenAI

Rather than opting for a formal phrase like _Sora Launches Today_, OpenAI chose a more conversational, casual tone with _Sora 2 is here_. This strategy helps the brand appear approachable and personable, steering clear of the sense of detachment that AI products sometimes evoke. The straightforward style also gives the news a fresh, on-trend, and practical feel, heightening enthusiasm around this model.

![Card content with a cute cloud on a starry background. Beneath it is the text "Sora 2 is here."](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2025/11/25/screenshot-2025-11-05-at-54952pm.png)

_OpenAI’s introduction of Sora 2_ (Sora 2 is here) _used a casual tone to humanize and build excitement for this AI model._

### Conclusion

Informative microcopy acts as the backbone and scaffolding for most interfaces, guaranteeing that users know what to do and how to do it. Make sure your writing delivers the essential information as effectively as possible by using the 3 C’s framework.
