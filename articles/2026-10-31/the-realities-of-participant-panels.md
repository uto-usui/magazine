A four-part guide to advocating for, building, and sustaining research panels, drawn from 25 industry interviews.

#### **About this project**

This piece is part of the [ResearchOps community’s](https://researchops.community/) ongoing volunteer project exploring the Participant Lifecycle. Based on community feedback, this series focuses on participant panels.

Panels are often seen as a cornerstone of scaling research because they promise to solve one of its biggest headaches: recruiting participants. But like most initiatives, they succeed less often than you might hope, and the path to making them look easy is long, winding, and not entirely within your control.

As diligent researchers, we started with desk research. Content on panels has exploded in recent years (see Kate Towsey’s [Research That Scales](https://rosenfeldmedia.com/books/research-that-scales/)), and we figured a roundup of existing research would be a great start. While we found plenty of articles about panels, most of it stayed high level and almost all of it was written by vendors with a product to sell. We found few case studies and virtually no unbiased guidebooks on how to navigate the decisions teams actually face when building a panel.

To fill that gap, we interviewed 25 practitioners from the ResearchOps community who had set up a panel, managed one, or often both. This series draws on their experiences across a range of panels, including B2C, B2B, government, and non-profit or third sector.

#### **This Was Supposed To Be A ‘How-to’ guide**

We started with a simple goal: write a how-to guide for building and running a participant panel, grounded in the experiences of real researchers. A small convenience-sample survey surfaced consistent themes around the objectives, challenges, and success metrics of panels, and we planned follow-up conversations to add context, color, and detail.

But then we talked to people. As expected, many echoed the core themes from our survey and desk research. What we didn’t expect was how much each researcher’s context and constraints revealed a bouquet of experiences rather than a repeated linear path.

So this series won’t give you a single playbook for building a panel. Instead, it shares the stories practitioners told us, in the hope they help you tackle your own mix of challenges and constraints.

#### **What Do We Mean by Panel?**

Ask ten researchers to define a panel and you’ll get ten answers, but most converge on something like this: _“a set of potential respondents who have declared that they will cooperate for future research projects.”_ That’s useful to hold onto, because it names the one thing every version of a panel shares: access. Beyond that, size, audience, and management style all vary.

That variance is wide enough that “panel” can mean a plain spreadsheet you email twice a year, or something with screening, profiling, and dedicated tooling behind it. Both get called a panel, and both can be ‘good’.

As one researcher put it: _“if you had a customer list and you were just sending out a regular survey every once in a while, you can do that, and you can call it a panel — but it’s not leveraging what a panel can be.”_ Where you land on that spectrum is a resourcing decision, not a definitional one.

**General panel fundamentals tend to include:**

-   Participants have consented to and expect repeat contact
-   Their utility outlives a single project
-   Some level of baseline data gets collected up front for screening and targeting
-   The relationship exists primarily for the organization’s research purposes

**What Isn’t Covered Here**

-   **Communities:** We consider panels to be fundamentally hub-and-spoke, as in one organization developing individual relationships with multiple individuals. When panelists start interacting with each other, it moves closer to being a ‘community’. While in practice communities and panels can overlap, this paper does not cover communities.
-   **Third-Party Panels:** Most of these stories reflect ‘owned’ panels that a researcher has set-up and grown. Though there are third-party providers in this space that can help fast-track panel creation, this paper is designed to support anyone creating their own panel.
-   **Customer Advisory Boards:** Customer Advisory Boards are leveraged similarly to panels, but the nature of the relationships, size, and duration tend to differ from a standard panel. For that reason, we do not broach advisory boards in this paper.
-   **Synthetic Panels:** Synthetic panels are a newer (and still hotly debated) form of research. This project doesn’t touch on synthetic research, digital twins, or any other hotly debated customer-like panels.

**There is No ‘One’ Way**

The zone of success for a panel is defined by three forces: your participants, your organization, and the resources you can marshal to create and sustain the panel. To succeed with the panel, you need to find a way that works for all three.

Throughout this series you’ll hear from researchers on how they navigated real-world constraints. Every quote and case study is a real conversation, not a composite. The point isn’t that you should do exactly what any one of them did. Rather, we hope you use their stories to better define and navigate your own constraints.

#### **How This Series is Organized**

The articles are organized into a multi-part series that roughly aligns with the phases our experts encountered. In practice, your experience may not line up one-for-one with the order we have laid out. You may need to bounce back and forth as you have the necessary conversations and build support and buy-in.

[**Participant Panels: Why Research Teams Build Them (and When They Should Buy Instead)**](https://medium.com/@thomas.n.anne/participant-panels-why-research-teams-build-them-and-when-they-should-buy-instead-157b1b8e4060?sharedUserId=thomas.n.anne) explores some of the different drivers behind the panels our research professionals were involved with.

[**Setting Up Your Research Panel**](https://medium.com/@thomas.n.anne/setting-up-your-research-panel-2d0d2425a390?sharedUserId=thomas.n.anne) focuses on the practical considerations that are frequently encountered when starting a panel from scratch — the stories might help you put in place a plan before you encounter these yourself!

[**Managing and Maintaining a Healthy Panel**](https://medium.com/@thomas.n.anne/managing-and-maintaining-a-healthy-research-panel-cc63af0b2a1f?sharedUserId=thomas.n.anne) focuses on ongoing panel management — once birthed, how do you keep it alive. Again, lots of different approaches to some fairly consistent challenges are told.
