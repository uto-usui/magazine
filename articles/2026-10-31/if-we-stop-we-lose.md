It’s been a rather _morale draining_ era in the making websites industry since the wider tech industry decided to gamble everything on LLMs. We at Piccalilli see it amongst the community and _feel it_ ourselves.

You’ll also have noticed the velocity of posts has slowed down somewhat on this website with no new courses announced either. There’s a couple of reasons for that. First, [the numbers have taken a dramatic tumble](https://bell.bz/in-response-to-the-death-of-web-development-education/) — [industry-wide](https://molily.de/web-dev-education/) — but also, aside from being very busy in [the studio](https://set.studio/), we’ve been _tired_. Swimming against this particular current will do that, even for folks like us that have _always_ operated like that.

Something else you might have noticed is that a lot of other educators have gone a lot _quieter_ this year too. I’m writing this post today to hopefully try and fix that.

## [A shot in the arm](#a-shot-in-the-arm)

I had a delightful conversation with [CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/) head honcho, [Geoff](https://geoffgraham.me/), yesterday. Aside from the excellent vibes as a result of CSS-Tricks [being back as an independent publisher](https://css-tricks.com/css-tricks-has-a-new-owner/), I started in a rather glum mood. After spending a few minutes listening to Geoff’s exciting plans though I really perked up.

We’re nowhere near CSS-Tricks here, but we’ve always looked up to them as an example of what years and years at the top of your game looks like. Sure, the DigitalOcean acquisition didn’t work out, but the fact that the publication is not only back, but well up for the fight against the supposed death of web education is really inspiring to me. I _needed_ that win, as did [many others](https://wil.to/posts/taking-back-css-tricks/).

I needed it so much, it’s spurred me on to write this article to hopefully give us _all_ a shot in the arm.

## [There is no inevitable future and education will always matter](#there-is-no-inevitable-future-and-education-will-always-matter)

A comment I see all the time is “we don’t need articles and courses because we can just ask an LLM now”, which I guess is true, but I could also eat an entire can of spray cheese. It ain’t gonna nourish me!

AI technologies have given anyone the ability to bring their ideas to life, but to produce a high quality end product that _actually works_ for the people it needs to work for requires experience, specialist skills and creativity. That _is_ a reality, which aside from Silicon Valley’s best efforts, is why there are still working developers out there, regardless of “frontier model” power. Human developer’s experience is _necessary_ because LLM outputs still (somehow, after hundreds of billions of investment) fall short.

Let me repeat that: their experience is _necessary_. Who inevitably contributed to that experience? Yep: educators.

## [Let’s get to work then](#lets-get-to-work-then)

This article is mainly addressed to fellow educators, but also to people that write on their personal blogs. **You must keep going because people still need us**. If we don’t, we lose.

The LLM era is opening up an enormous skills gap that’s only going to get wider as the industry continues to pull up the ladder, eliminating entry level positions. We’re needed to help people really understand how all of this stuff _works_, so even if they are at [the behest of LLM mandates](https://piccalil.li/blog/are-peoples-bosses-really-making-them-use-ai/), they can produce better outputs.

The technology is going nowhere — that toothpaste is long out of the tube — but how many other hype cycles have underwhelmed in the end?

The economics of the AI industry simply do not work and the technology — albeit ever-present in development — will fall into its actual place eventually, as a [niche tool](https://bell.bz/the-logical-destination-of-llms/).

With all of this in mind, we’re fighting back too, so [I’m opening up the guest authoring on this site again](https://piccalil.li/guest-authoring/), [just like Geoff is over on CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/guest-writing/). I’m determined to get this website _cooking_ again and I would love to share _your_ words with the rest of the industry.

Let’s buck up our ideas and get cracking, everyone. There’s lots to teach and I know we’ve got plenty of fight left in us.

To quote our very own [Mat Marquis](https://wil.to/js4e/newsletter/mat-update-22/):

> You and me, we’re not done until we say we’re done

Don’t let the bastards grind you down.
