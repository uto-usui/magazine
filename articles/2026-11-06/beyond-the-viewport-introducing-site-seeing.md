We care about responsive design while building a web product, and this means working with media queries or container queries to have a robust end result. However, few of us think about the big picture: how a website is used by real users in real contexts. There are so many variations other than resizing the browser window or viewing a design on mobile and desktop.

For this article, I will reference a design I made for testing purposes (Yes, I designed it by hand, if you’re wondering, and I took the photo in my home coffee corner. Weird, right?)

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/koob-bon.BaQy2BzM_ZVkvNx.png)

I imagined a simple tool where I can test how a design will look and behave in different contexts, so **Site Seeing** was born. With this tool, you can see how a web page will behave in different contexts that force unusual viewport sizes.

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/site-seeing.DQqxnNFV_ZHJLPL.png)

Let me know what you think!

## Let’s take a look

The tool lets you pick different modes or contexts for how a user might view a website. For example, here is the page with developer tools open.

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/devtools.DmBLyYhm_1M06Cv.png)

Or the design with vertical tabs.

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/vertical-tabs.CMlhmHyb_9Rakk.png)

Or even more complex, with both vertical tabs and developer tools.

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/vertical-tabs-devtools.CDkdWHxa_Zemnqt.png)

…and stuff like print styles!

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/print.B3AIuiBP_19A8lE.png)

These are just a few of the contexts the tool shows. I think this is much more important than just taking care of the viewport size. This tool is helpful for designers, product managers and developers who care about their craft.
