## Superior Type’s [Heaven](https://t-bi.link/superiortype-heaven) is a brutalist grotesque built on round shapes that are too big for their space. Drawn by type designer Petr Kucera, it comes as four families – Heaven 01, 03, 06 and 09 – each with 18 styles from Thin to Black, including italics, in Latin and Cyrillic. A SuperVariable version lets you set the weight to any point between the soft 01 and the boxy 09.

 

“At [Superior Type](https://t-bi.link/superiortype), we are trying to push fonts to extremes,” Kucera tells us. “So in addition to extreme thin and black variants, we are adding one more concept where we push our new fonts. We have a bunch of them in progress and executing them one by one, depending on the current state and vibe. Heaven has this soft-to-boxy variable axis.”

The typeface started close to Heaven 03. “Just try to imagine 03 as a blown-up balloon fitted inside a box that is a bit smaller than the balloon,” Kucera explains. “Those rounded curves copy the box shape but still stay round in the corner of the box.” From there, he shifted the balance between round and rectangular forms in both directions. “After I pushed far enough I got to the point where it stopped, which is now 01 and 09,” Kucera notes. If the balloon is blown up “bigger and bigger until it fills those corners, it becomes Heaven 09.” The four families, Heaven’s static stops, were then chosen the same way a type designer chooses the weights of a typeface. “You set up, print and look through more than a healthy amount of variations, and then slowly destroy those that are not different enough to speak for themselves.”

“Every letter made from diagonal lines had to be rethought: is it possible to make it round without losing too much readability?” Kucera reveals. “That’s why ‘K,’ ‘R,’ ‘W,’ ‘X’ and ‘Y’ got the upgrade to round.” Heaven comes with alternate letters that you can switch in. “The ‘m,’ ‘n’ and also ‘M’ and ‘u,’ I did with a specific goal in mind: to make Heaven 01 even more round, to combat the boxiness of 09 and keep them a bit more even in this shape battle,” he tells us. Asked what makes Heaven brutalist, Kucera points to the ‘K’ and ‘X,’ in upper and lower case, and to an alternate ‘M’ and ‘m’ that come from flipping the ‘W’ and ‘w.’ “I think there are a bunch of brutal – concept-wise – non-negotiable letterforms inside the font.”

“I think the character of each variation is strong enough to hold a logotype, but the regular weights are also balanced enough that I dare say they can handle long enough text to describe a company on its website,” Kucera notes. “I would love to see some designers grab one or two letters from 09 and the rest from 01 and make a custom typeface for a company font and logotype.”

Superior Type also has two serif text typefaces built as extensive SuperVariable families, like Heaven. “If any studios or designers are interested in testing them on upcoming projects, we’d love to hear from you,” Founder & Director Vojtech Riha tells us. “We’re happy to share preview versions ahead of release and see how they work in real-world use.”
