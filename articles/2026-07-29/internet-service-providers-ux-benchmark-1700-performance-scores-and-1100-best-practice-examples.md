The **overall UX** performance of the ISP sites is “mediocre,” with 1 site just on the border of “poor”.

ISP sites typically offer a narrow set of services, but these services come with complex eligibility rules and layered cost structures — placing **added pressure** on clarity and structure throughout the user journey.

Most sites provided strong initial navigation and product descriptions, but many **failed to support decision-making** where it mattered most: in the Plan Matrix and during application.

The Plan Matrix, **a critical comparison tool**, was frequently hindered by dense layouts, missing feature links, and a lack of supporting tools like FAQs or live chat.

Meanwhile, the application flow **often broke down** due to vague pricing, unclear progress indicators, and form-level friction.

Collectively, **these issues undermined** users’ ability to confidently select and apply for services, despite the relatively small product catalog.

For most ISP sites, improving clarity, guidance, and transparency in these high-stakes areas **would yield significant UX gains**.

As this benchmark shows, with **no sites** delivering even a “decent” or better overall user experience, there is plenty of room for improvement.
