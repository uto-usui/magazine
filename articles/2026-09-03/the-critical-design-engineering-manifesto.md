## Explainer

In 2011, Julian Oliver, Gordan Savičić and Danja Vasiliev published the Critical Engineering Manifesto, urging engineers to treat the technologies we depend upon as objects of study, suspicion, and intervention.

Fifteen years later, I’ve remixed their manifesto for the Critical Design Engineer: a person who wishes to critically inhabit the space between software design and engineering.

I have created this manifesto as a set of guardrails for myself as I navigate agentic engineering capabilities as a software designer. The structure, numbering, and cadence belong to Oliver, Savičić and Vasiliev. Where possible, I have tried to maintain kernels of their ideas in each statement.

The manifesto’s source text lives in version control, published as a [public Github Gist](https://gist.github.com/pipshea/5dec1e877d01cf67f36f19ef50af78e4), under the same GNU Free Documentation License as the original. It carries a commit history, an author signature, and an open invitation to fork.

## Manifesto

0. The Critical Design Engineer considers code to be a design material, and works it directly.

1. The Critical Design Engineer considers any tool that generates on their behalf to be both a capability and a threat. The greater the dependence on generated code, the greater the need to read, question and expose its workings.

2. The Critical Design Engineer recognises that proximity to code is not transparency; the source is itself a representation, and closeness must never be mistaken for understanding.

3. The Critical Design Engineer designs seams — not seamlessness — so the user can understand, configure, and contest.

4. The Critical Design Engineer looks beyond the awe of AI generation to the authored context that steers it.

5. The Critical Design Engineer recognises that code records what was decided, not why. So they write the why into the repository.

6. The Critical Design Engineer knows that ‘agent’ describes socio-technical dynamics involving models, parameters, tokens, reinforcement loops, protocols, products, networks, devices and bodies.

7. The Critical Design Engineer inhabits the space between generation and shipping, acting rapidly to hold influence where the codebase is still open to change.

8. The Critical Design Engineer looks to the history of critical design, speculative design, adversarial design and their discontents, and carries their strategies out of the gallery and into the repository, where critique must survive contact with use.

9. The Critical Design Engineer accepts authorship of consequence, not only of intent. To push is to sign your name.

10. The Critical Design Engineer considers the merge to be the most desirable and most dangerous form of exposure.

Remix of _The Critical Engineering Manifesto_ (Julian Oliver, Gordan Savičić & Danja Vasiliev, 2011). Published under the GNU Free Documentation License V1.3, after the original.

## Influence register

Thinkers and makers whose work shaped the manifesto’s statements.

-   Bainbridge, Lisanne: “Ironies of Automation” (1983); the paradox that judgement matters more as making automates. Statement 5.
-   Bleecker, Julian: “Design Fiction” (2009); objects that tell stories about possible futures. Statement 8.
-   Boer, Laurens & Donovan, Jared: “Provotypes” (2012); provocation over evaluation. Statement 8.
-   Chalmers, Matthew (and Ian MacColl): seamful design (2003); revealing rather than hiding the seams. Statement 3.
-   Chun, Wendy Hui Kyong: _Programmed Visions_ (2011); “sourcery,” source code mistaken for transparency. Statement 2.
-   Cross, Nigel: “Designerly Ways of Knowing” (1982); design as a distinct epistemology. Statement 0.
-   Dean, MC: _Designpowers_ (2026; github.com/Owl-Listener/designpowers); a design workflow built entirely from markdown skills and agent definitions. Authored context as a material that steers agent behaviour. Statement 4.
-   DiSalvo, Carl: _Adversarial Design_ (2012); agonism, making conflict contestable. Statements 8 and 10.
-   Dunne, Anthony & Raby, Fiona: _Speculative Everything_ (2013); design that reinforces how things are versus design that poses a question. Statement 8.
-   Fuller, Matthew: _Behind the Blip: Essays on the Culture of Software_ (2003); “critical software” that runs and critiques at once. Statement 8.
-   Fuller, Matthew (ed.): _Software Studies: A Lexicon_ (2008); software as an object of cultural and critical analysis. The field the manifesto works within.
-   Galloway, Alexander R.: _The Interface Effect_ (2012); the interface as threshold and generative friction rather than transparent window. Statement 3.
-   Krug, Steve: _Don’t Make Me Think_ (2000); the seamless experience orthodoxy the manifesto pushes back against. Statement 3.
-   Lessig, Lawrence: _Code and Other Laws of Cyberspace_ (1999; rev. as _Code: Version 2.0_, 2006); code as an instrument of governance. Statement 9.
-   Löwgren, Jonas & Stolterman, Erik: _Thoughtful Interaction Design_ (2004); the digital as design material. Statement 0.
-   Mackenzie, Adrian: _Wirelessness_ (2010); technology as a distributed field of relations rather than a discrete device. Statement 6.
-   Narayanan, Arvind & Kapoor, Sayash: “AI as Normal Technology” (2025) and “Why AI hasn’t replaced software engineers, and won’t” (2026); the decide-execute-deliver sandwich, value migrating upward. Statements 4, 5, 10.
-   Nygard, Michael: the Architecture Decision Record (2011); the why as its own artifact. Statement 5.
-   Oliver, Julian; Savičić, Gordan; Vasiliev, Danja: “The Critical Engineering Manifesto” (2011). The source text; structure, numbering, cadence, and its FOSS culture commitments.
-   Pope, Richard: “Designing the seams, not seamless design” (2025); transparency at the point of use. Statement 3.
-   Prado, Luiza & Oliveira, Pedro: “Cheat Sheet for a Non- (or Less-) Colonialist Speculative Design” (2014); whose futures speculative design imagines, and whose it does not. Statement 8.
-   Rittel, Horst: design rationale and the IBIS method (1970); the why as capturable, not merely implicit. Statement 5.
-   Schön, Donald: _The Reflective Practitioner_ (1983); the back-talk of materials. Statement 0.
-   Star, Susan Leigh (with Karen Ruhleder): infrastructure and “when is infrastructure” (1996, 1999). Statements 7 and 9.
-   Suchman, Lucy: _Plans and Situated Actions_ (1987), _Human-Machine Reconfigurations_ (2007); plans as resources for action, not determinants of it; the human-machine boundary as continually reconfigured. Statements 4 and 6.
-   Ullman, Ellen: _Close to the Machine_ (1997); code as intimacy and seduction. Statement 0.
-   Weiser, Mark (and John Seely Brown): “The Coming Age of Calm Technology” (1996); “beautiful seams.” Statement 3.
-   Winner, Langdon: “Do Artifacts Have Politics?” (1980); design choices legislate. Statement 9.
