How we decide

-   **Shows you ads:** the app shows you ads, so your attention is what it sells.
-   **Ads follow you:** by default, the app uses what you do to pick the ads you see, here or elsewhere.
-   **What you write trains AI:** by default, what you type or upload is used to train the company's AI.
-   Apps count by typical time spent in them each day. We never give any kind of app extra weight.
-   Only time looking at the screen counts. Music in the background doesn't.
-   We score the default settings. If a company lets you turn something off, we say so.
-   Google doesn't train its AI on Search history from school accounts.
-   Muse may still share de-identified lessons from your chats with Meta even with training off.
-   Turning off AI training protects what you write from now on. It doesn't remove what was already used, and some companies still keep chats flagged for safety.
-   Rules differ where you live. We describe the U.S. For example, Apple Maps shows ads only in the U.S. and Canada, and in the EU apps can't use profiling to target ads at kids, so TikTok and Snapchat for kids only show them ads.
-   If we found nothing taken whether you pay or not, we don't ask. Those apps count as paid with your wallet.
-   We never put dating apps in your receipt or link by name, because just using some of them reveals private things about you.
-   Dating apps count 20 minutes a day, split across the ones you pick. This is our estimate.
-   Paying for a dating app usually removes the ads. If we found nothing else taken, it counts as paid with your wallet, the same rule as every other app.

We use typical daily times, not yours. If you use several apps of one kind, we split that time between them. The only question that changes it is whether you work at a computer, because that is where most browser time happens.

We only label what a company's own settings or policies say, or what independent tests show. Customer means you pay and we found nothing taken, not that a company is safe. Promises can change.
