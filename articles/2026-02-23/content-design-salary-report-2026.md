Your years of experience and your technical skills are major factors, but they aren't the only ones. Three specific levers determine where you fall on that salary table.

### Industry: Big Tech vs. Startups

The industry you choose dictates your risk profile and your pay structure.

**Big Tech (FAANG/MAMAA)** — Companies like Google, Amazon, and Netflix offer the highest stability and the highest Total Compensation. Their base salaries are strong, but their RSU (stock) packages are liquid cash. You can sell them the moment they vest.

**Startups (Seed to Series B)** — Startups often cannot match Big Tech on base salary. A Senior Content Designer at a Series B startup might make $140,000 base compared to $180,000 at Google. But they offer equity options. If the company exits or IPOs, that equity could be worth millions. Or it could be worth zero. You're betting on the future value of the company.

### Job Title: UX Writer vs. Content Designer

This is a common debate, but the data is clear. Roles titled "Content Designer" or "Product Designer (Content)" consistently track higher in compensation surveys than "UX Writer." We covered this distinction in depth in our guide to [UX writing vs. content design](https://uxwritinghub.com/ux-writing-vs-content-design/).

Why? Because "Writer" implies service — someone who comes in at the end to polish the words. **"Designer" implies strategy** — someone involved from the discovery phase. Hiring managers perceive Content Designers as having a wider scope of influence, so they allocate deeper budgets for those roles. (See also: [UX writing vs. content strategy](https://uxwritinghub.com/ux-writing-vs-content-strategy/) — another title distinction worth understanding.)

## Understanding Total Compensation (TC) for Writers in Tech

When you read a salary report on [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/content-designer-salary-SRCH_KO0,16.htm), you're often seeing only one piece of the puzzle: **Base Salary**.

But in tech, the real wealth generation happens through **Total Compensation (TC)**. This is the sum of your Base Salary, Annual Bonus, and Equity (RSUs or Stock Options). For Senior and Principal roles, equity can make up 30–50% of your annual income.

If you only negotiate the base salary, you leave tens of thousands of dollars on the table. Always ask about the RSU refresh policy and the vesting schedule. A $140K base offer with $40K in liquid stock is far better than a $150K base offer with zero equity.

## FAQs About Content Design Salaries

How much do content designers make at Google? +

According to 2026 data from [Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/companies/google/salaries/product-designer/title/ux-designer) and verified offer letters, a **Level 4 (L4) Content Designer** at Google earns a Total Compensation (TC) of approximately **$180,000–$210,000**. This includes base salary, annual bonus, and RSUs. A **Level 5 (Senior) Content Designer** can expect a TC package ranging from **$260,000 to $310,000**. Google's Senior UX Writer base salary alone ranges from $138K–$202K based on current open listings.

Do technical writers make 6 figures? +

Yes, absolutely. Experienced technical writers in specialized fields like API documentation, cloud infrastructure, or developer experience often out-earn generalist UX writers. Senior Technical Writers at major tech firms routinely earn base salaries between **$130,000 and $170,000**, with total compensation packages exceeding $200K. The ceiling can sometimes be lower than Product Design leadership, but the floor is exceptionally high due to the scarcity of technical talent.

Are tech writers in demand? +

Demand for "Developer Experience" (DevEx) writers is surging. As every company becomes a platform company, they need clear, accurate documentation for their APIs and SDKs. In 2026, there are **440+ open content design roles** at any given time, with **116 new positions posted every week**. Companies like [Amazon](https://www.levels.fyi/companies/amazon/salaries/copywriter/title/ux-writer) and [Meta](https://www.levels.fyi/companies/facebook/salaries/graphic-designer/title/content-designer) consistently hire for these roles. While marketing content roles fluctuate with the economy, technical writing roles remain stable because bad documentation breaks products.
