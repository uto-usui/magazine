## Articles

[Accessibility getting dropped in the process](https://buttondown.com/nic-steenhout/archive/accessibility-getting-dropped-in-the-process/) (12min) Developers can say “this can be implemented accessibly,” after checking the design, and we might still end up shipping a feature with missing alt text and broken focus in the final product. Nic Steenhout argues the real problem isn’t a lack of accessibility knowledge, but ownership that never gets carried forward between design, dev, and QA. What needs to change? We need to find ways to carry forward the accessibility requirements at each stage: write accessibility into acceptance criteria, shared component libraries, and checkpoints throughout the process, not just a final audit.

[Enterprise UX at the limit: Why small patterns determine whether software and AI can scale](https://www.futurice.com/blog/enterprise-ux-patterns-ai-scale) (12min) Defining and documenting patterns, implementation constraints (anything that will make interactions predictable) is the key to consistency in enterprise products. Especially for highly re-used patterns: navigation, filters, data-heavy views, etc. The pattern documentation must be both human and machine-readable. Documentation is not enough: we need advocacy, clean ownership and a strong governance to enforce those rules. Felix Hohl also argues that standardized behavior and predictability will help agentic AI and accessibility. I can’t disagree here, even though it’s quite problematic that we now bundle AIs and disable people’s needs together.

Last but not least, I love this part: “Enterprise power users often need to see hundreds of data points at once. Forcing a “clean” look can break a professional’s workflow. The goal is not the prettiest solution. It is the most functional one.”. No notes, can we print it and put it in the office please?

[This Extremely Rare Neurological Condition Makes Faces Appear Distorted or ‘Like a Demon’](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/this-extremely-rare-neurological-condition-makes-faces-appear-distorted-or-like-a-demon-180984015/) (10min) the human brain is a marvel, and for the first time, scientists have recreated what one patient suffering from prosopometamorphopsia, or PMO, sees when he looks at faces.

## Interesting websites

[Why some people mow a lawn better than others](https://pudding.cool/2026/06/mow/): a fun experiment and an interactive dataviz, where The Pudding tested over 30 000 people on a virtual mowing game. The conclusion: finding the optimal path is not about thinking longer, but at the right moment. Planning complex sections while mowing the simple ones is the key.
