Welcome to Source Material, a weekly series where the designers and creatives shaping culture share the references that shaped them. Our next guest is [Brent David Freaney](https://brentdavidfreaney.com/), the founder of [Special Offer](https://www.specialoffer.inc/) and designer behind [Charli XCX’s “BRAT”](https://www.specialoffer.inc/work/xcx-brat) and [Madonna’s “Confessions II.”](https://www.specialoffer.inc/work/madonna-confessions-ii)

Catch up on [Brent’s Config 2026 talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yz03ERsfDDM), where he walks through what it takes to design for cultural impact, from the process behind a single shade of green for Charli XCX's “Brat” to the systems thinking that holds together large-scale creative campaigns.

After the shoot, we caught up with Brent about his creative process:

**What were you making when you realized, “This could be my job”?**

I'd say it's the first website I made on [GeoCities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeoCities) when I was in high school. It was a [“The Real World: Seattle”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Real_World:_Seattle) fan page.

**What’s the first thing you do when you sit down to work?**

I keep an active digital library of design references. Just screenshots and images I like that I come across on social media, or that might've been shared with me, or a photo that I may have taken on the street. The first thing I do when I sit down to work is review the most recent additions to make sure I still like them, or see if I was acting crazy when I saved them.

**What’s a design object you’d take to your grave?**

My boarding pass collection.

**Whose work makes you jealous?**

[Paul Rand](https://www.paulrand.design/) and [Bruce Weber](https://bruceweber.com/).

**What current design trend are you tired of seeing?**

Stretched type.

**What rule do you break the most?**

Stretching type.

**What song do you put on to get out of a creative rut?**

[“Ginseng Strip 2002 by Yung Lean.”](https://open.spotify.com/track/7v3rmoy5jcn4h5UqwQyCM3)

**What’s a piece of feedback you resisted, and later realized was right?**

Creating a body of work is a series of small adjustments, not full 180s from project to project.

**What’s a part of your process that most people don’t know about?**

I always choose color from IRL objects. The process of color picking from the RGB spectrum inside a software program is a never-ending fool's errand.

**What’s the last thing you came across that made you want to go make something?**

This image.
