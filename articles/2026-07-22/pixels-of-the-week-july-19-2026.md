## Articles

[Discovery vs Delivery](https://buzzusborne.com/writing/discovery-delivery/) (10min) AI can be useful for the delivery part of the design process, the part where you rapidly iterate different solutions. It’s useful also if you don’t need a deep discovery phase, and the “good enough” solution will work. But, the valuable part of product work is in the discovery: where you go to talk to users, identify the problem, reframe, identify what to build and what to not build. Playing in this space is messy, harder to structure around AI because the qualities that produce great outcomes are far less deterministic, and much harder to measure.

[The case for an accessibility designer vibe coding when all his coworkers are also vibe coding](https://ericwbailey.website/published/the-case-for-an-accessibility-designer-vibe-coding-when-all-his-coworkers-are-also-vibe-coding/)  (10min)  I understand Eric’s conflict here: having ethical issues with LLMs, while being pushed to work with them on a professional level to vibe code is a complicated position. Still, LLMs help him enhance accessibility faster than even if he is not so good at writing JavaScript. But that’s another problem on its own: being able to do something, without the foundational skills, thanks to the black box that the LLMs is.

[Will agentic browsing scoring actually help accessibility for people?](https://cerovac.com/a11y/2026/05/will-agentic-browsing-scoring-actually-help-accessibility-for-people/) (10min) [The new agentic browsing scores](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/agentic-browsing/scoring) reuse Lighthouse’s accessibility audit score (among other things) to evaluate how well an agent will be able to parse your content. Kind of, I’m simplifying a little bit just so that you get context to understand the article. This includes the accessibility tree. But a perfect score doesn’t mean real accessibility. We’ve been in this situation before: people chasing scores, not trying to actually improve accessibility. So let’s be careful here! By Bogdan Cerovac

[10 unusual ways to use the empty notebooks you keep buying](https://amycatriona.substack.com/p/10-unusual-ways-to-use-the-empty) (7min) the Pet peeves is the start of a DeathNote, just saying.

[Push Things That Roll](https://www.atvbt.com/push-things-that-roll/) (7min) “Some projects are like pushing a big round rock across a hilly terrain. Others are like pushing a big cube across a hilly terrain.” Downhill the round rock rolls on its own, while the cube requires pushing the entire time. But, both feel hard when pushing uphill. Often, the successful projects are the ones with the big sphere. Yup, I can definitely relate to that!

[On the Difference Between Rest and Idleness](https://idle.news/blog/on-the-difference-between-rest-and-idleness/) (8min) Society and the wellness industry weaponized “rest”: it gets celebrated only when it serves a productivity end goal, aka, a tool to make you work even better afterwards. Yup yup yup.

## Interesting websites

[Screen toys](https://screen.toys/) having a bad day? You might want to try some of those simulations. I admit, [splitting firewood](https://screen.toys/firewood/) online was fun.  Also how about [summoning explosive pomeranians running across your screen](https://screen.toys/poms/)? [Tooooools.app](https://www.tooooools.app/) is a free minimalist and lo-fi effects for image and video processing online. You get effects like stippling, dots, patterns, edge, distort etc

[How to center an element horizontally and vertically](https://css-generators.com/center/) yup, 100 different answers to these CSS questions everyone had for years. Some are quite hacky, but, still, fun to explore just to test the limits of CSS, hehe.

[Animation Vocabulary](https://animations.dev/vocabulary) a small glossary of animation terms. It says you can use it to prompt AI. I say you can use it if, like me, you write CSS animations, and always forget the difference between ease-out and ease-in. By Emil Kowalski
