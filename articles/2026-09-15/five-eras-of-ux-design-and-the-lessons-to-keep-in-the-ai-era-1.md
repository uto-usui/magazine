> **[Your product didn’t get worse](https://blog.mastykarz.nl/your-product-didnt-get-worse/?ref=sidebar) →**  
> “Your product didn’t get worse. It still does the job, works the way your customers expect and gives them no compelling reason to switch. Then those customers start using agents elsewhere, and the standard around your product changes. They ask an agent to research a trip or organize some files. They use it to make a change in another product. Jobs that used to require opening an app and completing a workflow across several screens turn into a sentence.”
> 
> **[Burnt out on a three-day week](https://jennywanger.com/articles/burnt-out-on-a-three-day-week/?ref=sidebar) →**  
> “The most common advice I see for product leaders facing burnout is to rest. Take PTO. Meditate. Lighten the calendar. When rest doesn’t work, the next piece of advice is to quit and find a different company. In coaching, I’ve watched enough people follow both playbooks to know they don’t always fix the problem.”
> 
> **[Limit the number of details](https://design.lightspark.com/limit-the-number-of-details?ref=sidebar) →**  
> “The first half gets your attention. The second half is what makes it possible. Fewer details allow you to care more deeply about each one. By details, I don’t just mean the small visual decisions. Every feature, control, mode, state, exception, and bit of behavior becomes something a team has to design and someone has to understand. There’s only so much attention to go around. Same for time, taste, and patience.”
