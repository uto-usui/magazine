The 40+ insights address the Food Delivery & Takeout **online shopping experience**, spanning a breadth of topics: preferred platforms, order times, order modification types, tipping behavior, fee perceptions, fulfillment methods, delivery concerns, issue resolution, loyalty program enrolment, and AI assistance.

Some of the charts also explore **sub-segments** of the broader audience such as [people with dietary restrictions](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/FD083D?time_period=August+2026), [orders for larger groups](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/FD019O?time_period=August+2026), and [urban vs. suburban purchasing behavior](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/FD083L?time_period=August%202026).

-   Meal customizations and add-ons are common among online food shoppers
-   Unexpected fees are extremely discouraging and risk abandonment

## 1) 1 in 2 Online Food Shoppers Typically Place Their Orders Within 15 Minutes

![Horizontal bar chart showing the time online food shoppers report between deciding they want food and placing their order.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/178637/original/research-media-file-12059fb047ae747b0b979122b037e8dc.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

More than half (55%) of online food shoppers say they placed their last order within 15 minutes of deciding they wanted food (See [Quantitative Insight #FD009](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/FD009?time_period=August%202026) for an interactive version of this graph alongside key findings and actionable insights).

Food delivery and takeout sites and apps address an **urgent moment of need** for shoppers — hunger. And this is evident in our first finding:

According to our survey, more than half (55%) of online food shoppers say they placed their most recent order within 15 minutes of deciding they wanted food, including a notable 15% doing so in **less than 5 minutes**.

A **smaller subset (32%) report taking 16-30 minutes** to settle on what they wanted to eat, with only 12% remaining who took longer than 30 minutes.

This reinforces the idea that **food delivery decisions are largely fast and impulsive** rather than methodical and considered.

The urgency of online food orders also points to the importance of design that is **optimized for speed and simplicity**, in both the browsing stages as well as the purchasing flow.

Therefore, as shoppers consider their options, it’s important for online food sites and apps to **allow for [easy order editing](https://baymard.com/guidelines/1964-editing-meal-orders-in-the-cart)**, [minimize pages for food customizations](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2024-subpages-for-selecting-food-customizations2024), and simplifying the [date and time selector](https://baymard.com/guidelines/1944-designing-the-date-and-time-selector) in an effort to target the 5-15 minute window.

Similarly, once online food shoppers are ready to purchase, **friction should be minimized** during checkout.

Checkout flows **shouldn’t introduce** [new cross-sells](https://baymard.com/guidelines/579-implementing-cross-sells-in-the-checkout-flow), have [too many form fields](https://baymard.com/guidelines/668-number-of-form-fields), or serve [ambiguous errors](https://baymard.com/guidelines/722-using-adaptive-error-messages) if looking to move shoppers to purchase completion quickly.

Lastly, **allowing returning customers** to [easily reorder](https://baymard.com/guidelines/1888-adding-buy-again-to-the-cart) from the homepage and cart, as well as promoting [past purchases](https://baymard.com/guidelines/1893-past-purchases-in-search-results) in search results, can further speed up orders for regulars and encourage loyalty.

See [Quantitative Insight #FD009](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/FD009?time_period=August%202026) for an interactive version of this graph alongside key findings and actionable insights.

## 2) Meal Customizations and Add-Ons are Common Among Online Food Shoppers

![Pie chart showing whether food delivery & takeout shoppers added on items during their last order.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/178638/original/research-media-file-c5602f1b5e5353e88adcd97774c69749.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

![Pie chart showing whether food delivery & takeout shoppers did customizations on their last order.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/178639/original/research-media-file-c47666e53fbeb3f4d57f139b37fc5de1.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

1 in 2 online food shoppers reported selecting add-ons to their last order, and 1 in 3 reported customizing the order in some way. (See [Quantitative Insight #FD019](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/FD019?time_period=August%202026) and [Quantitative Insight #FD017](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/FD017?time_period=August%202026) for an interactive version of these graphs alongside key findings and actionable insights).

Online food orders **aren’t** always simple and quick.

Food delivery and takeout shoppers with dietary restrictions, personal preferences, and inclinations toward savings **may select add-ons** to complement their order, or customize the contents of their dish.

Our survey shows that **1 in 2 online food shoppers reported selecting add-ons** to their most recent order, such as turning their meal into a combo, indicating upsell opportunities are a routine part of the ordering experience rather than an exception.

Notably, nearly a quarter (22%) of shoppers say that at least some of their add-ons were **impulsive or spontaneous**, suggesting strong upsell opportunities if properly considered and implemented.

In addition, **1 in 3 shoppers say they customized their last order** in some way (such as removing, adding, or substituting ingredients), suggesting a meaningful minority actively engages with personalization options.

Together, this means that ordering interfaces must design for users who have **specific needs** beyond standard menu items, similar to how they would make requests to their waiter in a restaurant.

To be clear, standard meal options should still be **streamlined**, and online food services should ensure that default meal offerings are intuitive and well-curated, without requiring modification for those who do not need it.

However, should shoppers seek add-ons or customizations, those [options should be clear](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2010-bespoke-meal-customization-options-on-the-product-page), use [familiar interfaces](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2027-using-traditional-checkboxes-and-radio-buttons-for-selecting-food-customizations), [not placed on subpages](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2024-subpages-for-selecting-food-customizations), and be editable in the [cart](https://baymard.com/guidelines/1964-editing-meal-orders-in-the-cart), in order to **best align** with commonly observed user behavior.

Relatedly, it is **crucial to communicate** whether these customization or add-on options are required, and make sure the associated [price or savings incurred](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2013-making-it-clear-when-meal-customization-add-ons-are-optional-and-incur-additional-fees) are permanently visible in the interface.

As we will see in the following chart, pricing is a sensitive area for online food shoppers, so **clear cost communication is paramount** when it comes to customizations and add-ons.

See [Quantitative Insight #FD019](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/FD019?time_period=August%202026) and [Quantitative Insight #FD017](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/FD017?time_period=August%202026) for an interactive version of these graphs alongside key findings and actionable insights.

## 3) Unexpected Fees are Extremely Discouraging and Risk Abandonment

![Horizontal bar chart outlining the types of fees that would most likely stop shoppers from completing an order.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/178640/original/research-media-file-6c186f49293e75af830481ce40b0891a.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

Online food shoppers report that unexpect fees, regardless of type, are the most discouraging fee type that would likely stop them from completing an order. (See [Quantitative Insight #FD069](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/FD069?time_period=August%202026) for an interactive version of this graph alongside key findings and actionable insights).

As our **[ecommerce study on cart abandonment](https://baymard.com/blog/cart-abandonment-rate)** reports, extra costs are a significant barrier and reason for abandonment among online shoppers.

However, among food delivery and takeout shoppers specifically, the _type_ of fee **makes a marked difference** on their motivation to follow through with an order.

While commonly accepted and understood fees such as taxes are not a significant roadblock (17%), the many **other types of fees** associated with online food orders can cumulatively discourage someone from ordering.

Most importantly, unexpected fees regardless of type **leave the worst impression**, with 54% of shoppers saying they would likely stop from ordering if they came across these.

Secondly, service fees are the **next most discouraging** fee type (48%), suggesting that shoppers may be sensitive to charges that feel platform-imposed or disconnected from the core value of their order.

Beyond those, extra charges for customization (38%), delivery fees (37%), and small order fees (32%) also reportedly make a meaningful share of shoppers **reconsider**, indicating that predictable and order-linked costs are more tolerable than opaque or structural ones.

With that, it’s imperative to **surface applicable fees _before_ checkout**, displaying them [as early as possible](https://baymard.com/guidelines/732-essential-price-info-for-the-cart) in the ordering flow to reduce abandonment driven by checkout surprise.

Also, there remains an opportunity to **reframe service fees as a value**, where [recontextualizing service fees](https://baymard.com/guidelines/551-what-info-to-display-at-the-review-step) with clear explanations of what they fund can help reduce the perception that they are arbitrary.

See [Quantitative Insight #FD069](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/FD069?time_period=August%202026) for an interactive version of this graph alongside key findings and actionable insights.
