Back in August 2025, I asked the question “are people’s bosses really making them use AI tools?” I spoke to developers and designers about that and learned some [eye-opening truths](https://piccalil.li/blog/are-peoples-bosses-really-making-them-use-ai/). To add some context, I thought AI mandates were disproportionate because we’d already evaluated this technology in [the team](https://set.studio/) and determined it was pretty useful, but not worth pursuing when the priority of _our_ work is quality, not _volume_.

The value of the technology has to be incredibly high when you consider the _extremely high ethical cost_ too. This technology has [encouraged suicide](https://edition.cnn.com/2025/11/06/us/openai-chatgpt-suicide-lawsuit-invs-vis), [devastated communities with the data center build-out](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cy8gy7lv448o) and [displaced thousands of people’s careers](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-tech-layoffs-2026-companies-cutting-jobs/), which is a tiny sample of the harms, so that ethical cost is and always will be astronomical.

In 2025 into early 2026, the AI industry was in a boom. The media was full of coverage about how amazing the technology is, how transformative it is and how you’re going to get left behind unless you full embrace it.

Stories of token spend leaderboards were starting to fill the airwaves and developers spending billions of tokens a week was been celebrated by a [loud](https://bell.bz/the-extremely-loud-minority/) contingent of the developer community, especially. LinkedIn was also full of leaders _gushing_ about productivity gains. With that in mind, it’s understandable why managers and decision makers were thinking AI would be “the next big thing” for productivity gains and immediately make moves to make AI a requirement for the job.

It wasn’t long though, before the value of these mandates was being questioned. [Companies outside of tech were talking about the disappointing productivity and quality returns](https://www.apollo.com/wealth/insights-news/insights/daily-spark/no-signs-of-ai-payoff-outside-the-tech-sector). That’s continued ever since and yet, in tech especially, it seems like companies are just ploughing on, [generating as much volume as possible while their workers burn out](https://css-tricks.com/when-everything-has-changed/) and the ladder for entry level workers gets pulled even higher up.

Now, during what looks increasingly to be a bust period and very likely the long anticipated bubble burst, I actually think it’s the perfect opportunity for leaders who didn’t share my opinion originally to take stock and revisit their strategy.

## [Where to go from here](#where-to-go-from-here)

I’ve already [written about what I think should be done now](https://css-tricks.com/when-everything-has-changed/), so let me pick out the bit I think is most important for mangers and decision makers to read:

> Again, token subsidies will inevitably end. So, getting thousands of dollars of worth from a $200 a month subscription will be something you’ll look fondly back on. When hallucinations from AI tools are suddenly costing _thousands_, does it make sense to force your staff to produce as much _volume_ as possible? Of course not.
> 
> **My advice is to re-evaluate those prior decisions and invest saved costs _back_ into developing your workers instead**, while there’s still a hope of them not burning out completely. Make sure you include your workers in these decisions and discuss the impacts of mandates _on them_. It’s important for you to understand the impact of these sort of decisions.

I speak to a lot of different people in our industry — many of whom are _furious_ about how the last couple of years have gone. If I was to pick the most common gripe, it would be that there’s a culture of _embracing_ de-skilling. Even if _de-skilling_ is too much for your taste, there’s a culture that atrophying skills is fine because “we’ve never been able to produce this much volume before.” That volume is highly dependent on spending lots of money with AI providers.

David [summarises well here](https://dbushell.com/2026/11/25/web-industry/):

> What possible _positive_ future are we expecting by locking knowledge and workflow behind a chatbot subscription? How is this even a question that needs asking?

At the end of the day, it doesn’t matter how “good” AI tools get because the value of good developers is not volume. It’s quality, experience, creativity, curiosity and wisdom. **That’s the value**.

As I [recommended in my piece from last week](https://css-tricks.com/when-everything-has-changed/), we have to bring skill building back to the forefront of our work. We work in an industry that rapidly changes, where technological advancements transform how we work and browser capabilities improve at lightning pace. We spend a lot of time describing developers as engineers but don’t invest anywhere near enough on maintaining knowledge and skills like actual engineers do. This needs to change and the time to do it is now.

## [Get ahead of the curve](#get-ahead-of-the-curve)

The writing is on the wall. The AI industry is being held together by [circular financing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_build-out_financing) from a company, run by a CEO that _the President of the United States of America_ [effectively installed back into their position](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ckj05m2zdyryo), even after being a [national security risk](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6zxjx01vyleo) and [allegedly being behind an illegal trade of chip sales](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/20/california-investigates-nvidia-over-whether-chip-exports-ended-up-in-china). The [flagship IPO of Anthropic is in free-fall](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/stocks/anthropic-stock-falls-below-ipo-price-ceo-sells-280-million-in-shares) and then you need to consider that they — even after this downturn — are still on the hook for [over $400 billion in spending commitments that they cannot cancel](https://www.wheresyoured.at/exit-liquidity/#anthropic%E2%80%99s-s-1-%E2%80%94-and-q2-2026-%E2%80%94-tells-the-story-of-a-very-different-company-to-the-one-that-will-go-public-with-over-430-billion-in-non-cancellable-obligations:~:text=Anthropic%E2%80%99s%20S%2D1%20%E2%80%94%20And%20Q2%202026%20%E2%80%94%20Tells%20The%20Story%20Of%20A%20Very%20Different%20Company%20To%20The%20One%20That%20Will%20Go%20Public%2C%20With%20Over%20%24430%20Billion%20In%20Non%2DCancellable%20Obligations,-So). That’s not a healthy industry!

There’s no doubt that this bubble is going to burst. You could even argue that’s happening right now! So instead of waiting for the inevitable, I recommend that you get ahead of the curve, remove the AI mandates and relegate the technology back to a more appropriate place: being a tool that _can_ be used to assist humans to deliver good work if they feel the need to use it.

It’s also worth reevaluating open source models, which I think is the [logical destination of this technology](https://bell.bz/the-logical-destination-of-llms/). They bring their own infrastructure challenges with them, but if AI really _is_ a game changer in your company, they’re challenges worth solving. It seems dangerous betting on companies that are so at risk of failing, right? Why not cut them out now and replace their tools as a matter of urgency? The immense saved costs can be put _back_ into investing in your worker’s learning budgets.

At the end of the day, this is _our job_ as leaders. It’s _our job_ to react to changes and keep things running smoothly. It’s _our job_ to look at past decisions, determine they were incorrect and act accordingly **to correct them**. As I also [said before](https://css-tricks.com/when-everything-has-changed/):

> A big part of _our_ job, in fact, the _most_ important part, is to ensure that our workers are looked after. I’m not just talking about compensation and perks here. I’m talking about our responsibility to ensure that our workers’ skills are kept sharp, their professional curiosities are satisfied and above all else that they are happy in the work they are doing. Our workers are much more than “number go up” vehicles.

Use the facts from your organisation to determine whether this technology is actually appropriate _for your team_ and heck, we might get to the other side of this in better shape than we feared. If that feels like an impossible task, then [give me a shout](https://bell.bz/links/). I’m sure [we’ll](https://set.studio/) be able to help you find a way forward.

For workers reading this: keep going and do what _you_ can to keep your skill levels high. I know many of you feel like there is no end to this nightmare, but the nightmare is very much getting ready to roll the credits, as I see it.
