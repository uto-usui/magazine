## Learn how to use AI coding tools to build your own designs

Making software is supposed to be fun! And this six weeks is packed with the most fun I’ve had in my 15-year teaching career.

LLM-assisted coding tools can put you in the driver’s seat, working with the real materials of your craft. I’m seeing designers do things I didn’t think I’d ever get to see designers do.

For as long as I can remember, product designers have been pushed away from code. Most designers settled this question for themselves long ago—_code is not for me_. Now is a great time to reconsider that decision. You can have a relationship with what feels like real work without spending years mastering syntactical minutiae.

Working on a design with code is an act of shaping: you’re shaping something out of the raw materials yourself. And as you shape it, you can actually interact with it, get the feel of it. This means you can work on a user interface the way it really works.

As designers, we enjoy making things. Working with code is a fantastic opportunity to feel connected to the thing you’re making.

Read on to find out more about the course, but keep in mind that **the most important things that happen in my classroom** [**have nothing to do with the syllabus**](https://buttonschool.com/classroom/).

### What you’ll learn

Working with code as the raw material of software will change how you see design and what you can do with it.

## Prerequisites

-   ### No previous coding experience is required
    
    Any amount of knowledge will work in your favor, but the course is made for designers with little-to-no coding skills.
    
-   ### Curiosity about how things work under the hood
    
    The course leans into understanding—not just generating—so a desire to go beyond surface-level outputs will help you get the most out of it.
    
-   ### Experience designing real-world products
    
    You should be comfortable making product decisions and thinking beyond individual screens.
