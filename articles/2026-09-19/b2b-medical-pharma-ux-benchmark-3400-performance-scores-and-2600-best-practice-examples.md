The **overall UX** performance of the B2B Medical & Pharma sites ranges from “poor” to “mediocre”.

Here are just a few of the **overarching UX issues** we observed.

Product documentation and specification details were often **incomplete or inconsistent** between products, complicating comparison.

While the specifications that were present read clearly, the documents behind them were **frequently missing**, and the same attribute was often labeled differently from one product to the next (see guidelines [#2542](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2542-manufacturer-s-datasheets) and [#839](https://baymard.com/guidelines/839-making-specs-consistent-and-uniform)).

Furthermore, product prices were **often unclear** (see guidelines [#792](https://baymard.com/guidelines/792-product-price-visibility) and [#817](https://baymard.com/guidelines/817-price-information-for-product-sold-in-multiple-amounts)), and [full order cost](https://baymard.com/guidelines/825-providing-a-total-order-cost), and [delivery timing](https://baymard.com/guidelines/802-where-and-how-to-display-delivery-information) were commonly absent from the product page buy section.

Search **offered little help** when a query was imperfect.

Sites handled exact product titles well, but did much less once a query or an entry was slightly off (see guidelines [#355](https://baymard.com/guidelines/355-handling-misspellings-on-search-results-pages) and [#358](https://baymard.com/guidelines/358-providing-alternative-search-queries)).

**Carts couldn’t be** saved, shared, or handed to another person to complete.

B2B users often **[need to collaborate](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2682-cart-collaboration-features-for-b2b-items)** with others on their orders, but nothing in the cart supported an order assembled by one person and placed by another.

As this benchmark shows, with **no sites** delivering even a “decent” or better overall user experience, there is plenty of room for improvement.
