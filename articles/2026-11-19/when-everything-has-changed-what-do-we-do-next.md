It’s been a… year, huh? I think the most common emoji I’ve seen this year is “Face with Bags Under Eyes” (🫩) and that says a lot. One thing is for sure: the industry we make a living in is in flux, regardless of the outcomes of the AI bubble. Let’s take a look at how we can try and navigate that situation as best we can. But first…

## The reality of where we currently are

Technology hyperscalers like Microsoft and Meta, along with AI startups like Open AI and Anthropic have transformed _everything_ about tech and the job we do. This is true regardless of if they fail or _somehow_ succeed in their quest to have everyone paying a subscription fee for their Large Language Model (LLM) technology, which is marketed to us as _AI_. I’ll refer to this technology as AI for the rest of this article for simplicity.

It’s not just tech jobs that have transformed either. Everywhere you go, you will see some _slop_, somewhere. Be it a flyer, website, a village poster or a food truck menu. Heck, [people are even using LLMs to work out what’s special about their _own children_](https://www.reddit.com/r/antiai/comments/1xfkxro/ai_tells_marissa_mayer_why_her_daughter_is_special/). The effects of this technology are far-reaching, way beyond our own industry.

The technology has hit us all in tech especially hard though. Mid-last year, [I did some investigating](https://piccalil.li/blog/are-peoples-bosses-really-making-them-use-ai/) because I suspected that so-called AI mandates were becoming increasingly popular and what I learned from conversations _then_ feels like some sort of utopia compared to _now_. In the space between that piece being published and _now_, we went from [token maxxing](https://fortune.com/2026/04/09/meta-killed-employee-ai-token-dashboard/) to companies [realizing that actually, this technology is extremely expensive and not returning the promised outcomes](https://www.apollo.com/wealth/insights-news/insights/daily-spark/no-signs-of-ai-payoff-outside-the-tech-sector).

The mandates have cooled, sure, but the demands to use AI to produce _more volume_, regardless of whether that volume is actually needed or not are still very much there. I’m in a fortunate position to have friendships with people all across the industry, from tech hyperscalers, to tiny little studios, and the stories I have heard from their work life are nothing short of horrifying. No one really seems to be happy with their work either.

This is such a long way away from even a handful of years ago where yes, there was a lot of problematic culture and outcomes from the tech industry, but people were generally quite happy with the work they were doing at least.

## The symptoms of where we currently are

The sudden urge to sprint _towards_ AI technologies over the last few years has had a devastating effect in so many places. The technology has [encouraged suicide](https://edition.cnn.com/2025/11/06/us/openai-chatgpt-suicide-lawsuit-invs-vis), [devastated communities with the data center build-out](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cy8gy7lv448o) and of course, [displaced thousands of people’s careers](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-tech-layoffs-2026-companies-cutting-jobs/). It’s worth noting that I’ve mentioned only a tiny sample of the harms there. If I listed them all, this article would be unbearably long and depressing.

Some harms I do want to touch on though are the effects on our communities in tech. The community spirit of open source and open knowledge was the foundation of the industry’s — especially the web industry’s — rapid success. At the root of everything there was people happy to share knowledge and **build everyone up _with_ them**. Those people still do exist — myself included — but being one of those people is increasingly difficult.

As the numbers go up in the AI world, the numbers [very much go down](https://bell.bz/in-response-to-the-death-of-web-development-education/) for independent educators, publishers and community conferences. There’s a developing and worrying culture of “I don’t need to skill up now because I can use AI for work”, so allow me to break down why that _reductive_ view of your work and your career is harmful in the long term.

## We are much more than code slingers

And don’t ever forget that! Let’s have a think for a second about what actually makes you a good developer, because typing characters in a text editor certainly isn’t it. What makes us good is experience, knowledge, nuance and above all else, _creativity_.

Look, I _get it_, AI really _can_ feel like it’s a miracle machine, but it’s important to take a wider view. Along with the [published studies on cognitive decline](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.00181v1) and the [widening skills gap](https://eu.usatoday.com/story/money/2026/02/27/ai-entry-level-work-jobs/88590459007/), it’s more important than ever to invest in **your brain** to keep your skills sharp because you’re not getting that **repetitive reinforcement that writing code every day gives you** anymore if AI tools do everything for you. There’s a reason that I often build most of my teaching around a linear build-along because the tangible nature of repeating patterns you’ve learned really helps that knowledge to sink in.

As always, this article is addressed to _everyone_, but I want to talk directly to founders (like [me](https://set.studio/#studio)), decision makers, and managers for a second. A big part of _our_ job, in fact, the _most_ important part, is to ensure that our workers are looked after. I’m not just talking about compensation and perks here. I’m talking about our responsibility to ensure that our workers’ skills are kept sharp, their professional curiosities are satisfied and above all else that they are happy in the work they are doing. Our workers are much more than “number go up” vehicles.

You _should_ be giving your developers more time to learn, rather than chasing endless _volume_. If AI enthusiasts are to be believed, there’s hours upon hours of time efficiencies created by this technology. Don’t fill that time with more volume. Instead, invest that time in developing your worker’s skills. It’s going to avoid burnout which yep, [daily LLM use will likely cause in the long term](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.10050).

## Sprinting towards LLMs and more volume carries a lot of risk

One thing that is increasingly clear is that the health of the AI industry is not good. The bubble has [already effectively burst in China](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/ai-bubble-burst-china) and more worryingly for economy enjoyers, the “picks and shovels” — AKA the chips industry in Korea — [has seen dramatic downturns](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/article/global-chip-sell-off-deepens-as-south-korean-market-plunges-024927049.html). This is not how an “inevitable” outcome is supposed to happen!

The U.S. AI industry is on very shaky ground too. [Circular financing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_build-out_financing), [unbelievable levels of debt](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/big-tech-will-fund-more-than-a-third-of-its-ai-investments-with-debt-in-2027-goldman-sachs-predicts-145136150.html?ref=wheresyoured.at) and a data center build out that simply [cannot keep up with the required power levels](https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2026/01/14/power-shortages-threaten-to-cap-datacenter-growth/4729136) to turn profits for hyperscalers and startups, mixed with a genuine hatred of the technology by the general public, creates enough risk in itself. Recent events, such as the [Democrats dominating the midterms causing AI stocks to drop](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/ai-data-center-stocks-drop-100424346.html) and [as well as Nvidia’s stock because of its CEO being caught up in a spying scandal](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6zxjx01vyleo) shine a light on the _fragility_ of the industry. A healthy industry doesn’t work like this.

With all that in mind, what would be a really silly thing to do? Yeh, you guessed it: put AI at the heart of everything in your business. Sure, there’s open source models — which I think is the [logical destination of the technology](https://bell.bz/the-logical-destination-of-llms/) — but how many companies are going to be willing to maintain their infrastructure needs? Not many. The low barrier of entry of commercial AI technology is the selling point, let’s be honest.

## That’s a lot of context, Andy. What can we do to make things better?

Above everything, **people** need to be invested in, _not_ pushed aside by technology like AI. As I mentioned earlier, it’s experience, knowledge, nuance, and creativity that sets humans far apart from AI. That technology does indeed keep evolving, but even the most ardent of AI supporters will concede that the rate of frontier model improvement is very much levelling out.

There’s never been a more important time to keep human skills sharp, so here’s some ideas on how to do that.

### Spend more time learning

This is not just directed at individuals but also at founders, decision makers and managers. Our industry has been historically excellent at investing in skilling people up and we need to bring that back.

Give staff the time and budget to _learn_, to develop their skills and to engage with the community at conferences. You already know the positive outcomes that brings, so **bring it back to your organization**. At the absolute worst case, it’ll make the usage of AI in your organization more efficient and when the [token subsidies](https://www.centific.com/blog/ai-token-consumption-costs) inevitably end, more cost effective.

### Re-evaluate AI mandate decisions

Again, token subsidies will inevitably end. So, getting thousands of dollars of worth from a $200 a month subscription will be something you’ll look fondly back on. When hallucinations from AI tools are suddenly costing _thousands_, does it make sense to force your staff to produce as much _volume_ as possible? Of course not.

**My advice is to re-evaluate those prior decisions and invest saved costs _back_ into developing your workers instead**, while there’s still a hope of them not burning out completely. Make sure you include your workers in these decisions and discuss the impacts of mandates _on them_. It’s important for you to understand the impact of these sort of decisions.

### Build stuff by hand

This advice is more aimed at developers, but stay paying attention, managers. As I said earlier, repetition (especially typing) reinforces new learning _and_ existing knowledge. The slower pace also allows your brain to _think_, which from a quality of output perspective, is imperative. Doing none of that atrophies skills over time.

I recently read Jenn Schiffer’s [great post](https://livelaugh.blog/posts/weird-web-october-2026/). She did the Weird Web October challenge and wrote this:

> \[…\] i encourage anyone who uses ai and/or vibe codes to consider doing an exercise like this without those tools as a way to challenge your brain and you can continue doing your regular work the way you do your regular work

I think that’s a very good idea, even if it’s messing around with a little side project — if you’ve got the energy from Producing Value all day, of course. Guess what bosses: this is another way you can make your staff happier, by giving them time to do this on the clock.

### Keep the soul of the industry alive and support independents

At the end of the day, you’ll [miss the soul when it’s gone](https://bell.bz/youll-miss-the-soul-when-its-gone/). It’s _not_ gone yet though. Far from it. Independents like CSS-Tricks, my publication, [Piccalilli](https://piccalil.li/), and [Smashing Magazine](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/) are trying hard to keep the soul of our industry alive by sharing high quality educational content with the community.

Most of this content is free at the point of entry, but it certainly isn’t free to produce. We all pay writers fairly to share their knowledge with the community and we are all _tiny_ organizations. We’re certainly not in this game to make huge profits, that’s for sure.

All of us _could_ do more though, but funding is the ultimate barrier. For both companies and individuals, [purchase courses](https://piccalil.li/courses/), [join the membership programs](https://css-tricks.com/memberships/), and purchase ad space. With the right financial support, publications like ours can be a _real force_ in pushing the good stuff back into our industry and bringing everyone up with us. **That’s what we do it for.**
