## Flight Shoppers Use a Variety of Sources When Looking for Flights

![Horizontal bar chart showing what sites, apps, or sources travelers used when searching for their last purchased flight.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/173695/original/research-media-file-f5fa20077df9a7f8000bb527546fec98.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

Airline-owned channels like websites (50%) and apps (29%) dominate flight search, but online travel agencies remain well utilized at 38%. (See [Quantitative Insight #FB017](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/FB017?time_period=March%202026) for an interactive version of this graph alongside key findings and actionable insights).

Flight shoppers often have to weigh a **variety of complex factors** when searching for a flight and therefore, look to multiple sources and tools to do so — sometimes more than one.

According to our survey, airline-owned channels dominate flight search, with **airline websites being by far the most used source** by flight shoppers in their last purchase (50%).

Combined with airline mobile apps (another 29%), this suggests **strong brand trust and preference for direct booking**.

That said, online travel agencies (OTAs) remain the **second-most-used resource** at 38%, and aggregators such as Google Flights and Kayak are used by 23% of respondents.

[Loyalty programs](https://baymard.com/blog/loyalty-program-ux-launch) are also **more significant in this industry**, with 19% saying that they search their travel rewards or loyalty portal.

All of this suggests that flight shoppers have a **broad spread of sources** to search for options.

These findings also indicate that shoppers tend to **look in more than one place**, not necessarily just the direct airline source, underscoring the importance of a strong, [prioritized search experience](https://baymard.com/guidelines/1866-prioritizing-the-booking-search-feature) on a site or app’s homepage.

If the search function’s discoverability, [autocomplete suggestions](https://baymard.com/guidelines/1870-suggestions-in-destination-autocomplete), or search results are **not clear**, users are likely to continue looking elsewhere.

Similarly, as flight shoppers consider and compare various factors, it’s important that **[pricing is clear](https://baymard.com/guidelines/452-price-clarity-in-product-lists-and-search-results)** across lists and search results, alongside other [consistent](https://baymard.com/guidelines/443-consistency-of-product-attributes-across-list-items-and-search-results) and [comparable attributes](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2281-category-specific-product-attributes), including [loyalty points and rewards balances](https://baymard.com/guidelines/3153-showing-points-and-rewards-balances-on-loyalty-programs-apps-or-mobile-homepages) when appropriate.

If users are not provided with clear, relevant, and comprehensive information right away, **other sites or services will provide it** for them.

## Three Quarters of Flight Shoppers Are Part of at Least One Airline Loyalty Program

![Pie chart showing whether flight shoppers say they are currently a member of any frequent flyer or airline loyalty program.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/173696/original/research-media-file-505085c89bb3143a089c643baa58e2cd.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

Membership in airline loyalty programs is common among travelers surveyed, with 78% saying they belong to at least one program, higher than the 60% seen in general B2C ecommerce. (See [Quantitative Insight #FB046](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/FB046?time_period=March%202026) for an interactive version of this graph alongside key findings and actionable insights).

Loyalty program participation differs across industries, but **flight shopping may be among the most widely used**.

Our survey shows that membership in airline loyalty programs is very high among travelers, with **78% saying they belong to at least one program**, notably higher than the [60% seen in general B2C ecommerce](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/LY004?time_period=2025).

Single-airline loyalty **dominates the landscape**, as 42% of respondents say they belong to a program for just one airline, outpacing multi-airline members (27%) by a wide margin and suggesting most shoppers focus on a primary loyalty relationship.

That said, a meaningful share of members remain disengaged, with 9% saying they belong to a program but do not actively use it, indicating that **enrollment alone does not translate to meaningful participation**.

With this high adoption rate in mind, information about loyalty programs **should be top of mind** across the entire flight shopping experience.

**The platform should** [show the points and rewards balance](https://baymard.com/guidelines/3153-showing-points-and-rewards-balances-on-loyalty-programs-apps-or-mobile-homepages) on the user’s account, with the [value of the program](https://baymard.com/premium/strategic-briefs/lyb11) being prominently highlighted.

**Other communication channels**, such as [loyalty emails](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2988-points-and-rewards-balances-in-loyalty-program-emails), should also include points and reward balances, and a history of [earned and redeemed points](https://baymard.com/guidelines/3141-showing-a-history-of-users-points-earning-and-redemption) should also be easily accessible to communicate the continued value of returning to a particular airline or service.

See [Quantitative Insight #FB046](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/FB046?time_period=March%202026) for an interactive version of this graph alongside key findings and actionable insights.

![Horizontal bar chart with % of flyers who bought other travel bookings with their flight ticket, segmented by flight type.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/173697/original/research-media-file-a4841b347622d46ccc97dd3e60ced700.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

Hotels are the most commonly co-booked add-on for both business and leisure travelers, but business travelers say they co-book hotels at a substantially higher rate (56%) than leisure travelers (35%). (See [Quantitative Insight #FB026B](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/FB026B?time_period=March%202026) for an interactive version of this graph alongside key findings and actionable insights).

Flight shoppers in our survey exhibited a **tendency to “bundle”** their most recent flight purchase with other travel-related bookings at the same time, including hotels, rental cars, travel insurance, or airport parking.

Interestingly, the frequency of **this behavior differs** depending on whether the trip was for business or leisure.

Hotels are the most commonly reported co-booked add-on for both business and leisure travelers, but **business travelers co-book hotels at a substantially higher rate (56%)** than leisure travelers (35%).

Adding to this, business travelers are **more likely than leisure travelers to add** a rental car (40% vs. 22%), travel insurance (26% vs. 14%), as well as airport transfers or parking (26% vs. 13%).

With that, **nearly half of leisure travelers (46%)** report making no additional bookings alongside their flight compared to only 18% of business travelers, indicating that leisure travelers are more likely to book travel components separately rather than all at once.

From a strategic standpoint, it’s therefore important to **offer bundling cross-sells contextually**.

Promotions and special offers should be [made visible by default](https://baymard.com/guidelines/581-costs-for-special-offers-and-promotions), but they shouldn’t be [pushed too aggressively](https://baymard.com/guidelines/579-implementing-cross-sells-in-the-checkout-flow), and they **may hold more [relevance if targeted](https://baymard.com/guidelines/578-adapting-cross-sells-to-the-user-s-context)** towards business flyers over leisure flyers, for example.
