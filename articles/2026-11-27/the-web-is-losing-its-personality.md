## Introduction

If you browse websites created in 2026, you will have a hard time distinguishing them from each other. The differences are hard to spot, and they’re getting harder to spot every day.

I will start with an abstract example.

In the following figure, can you spot the unique face?

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/unique-design-01.DKehLNk7_Z1juTAR.jpg)

You might be wondering, what unique face? Is there a mistake in the question? Nope. This is exactly what’s happening in web design right now. People think that their AI-generated design is unique, but it’s just another copy of what the AI agent already knows.

A website design should reflect its brand and values, not the LLM that produced it.

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/unique-design-02.CIBn_ont_yi42T.jpg)

On top of that, each of us has different cultures and thoughts, so this affects how we perceive and work on designs.

For example, I will use coffee as a topic to compare. Each one of us has our own way of drinking coffee. Take a look:

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/unique-design-03.BciZWA6V_ZcdUbo.jpg)

Just by looking at the coffee details, you will notice each face prefers a different way. This says a lot about where they came from. However, that can change over time.

For example, I started by drinking Turkish coffee, and now I’m a full-time home barista, and espresso has become a daily part of my routine.

Your design taste changes with your life, and that is what makes your work humane. AI doesn’t have a soul/life so that it won’t have that at all. The coffee thought is one of hundreds, if not even thousands, of ideas and experiences each one of us has.

I imagine it like the following figure where **the human experience is personal and based on life experiences. In contrast, the LLM one is more uniform due to it using the most common for a given problem**.

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/unique-design-04.Bwj6wZeV_xdrcr.jpg)

## Are you designing with AI?

If you’re one of the many who rely on AI to design for you, then you are missing a lot. I have one simple question for you: who will see and use the design you will build? Is it a human or an agent?

If you are designing for agents, you don’t need to design at all. I guess you design for people. Even if you use “UI skills” to improve the agent’s output, many people rely on the first shot and call it a day.

Take a look.

## Signs of one-shot AI design

Over time, we started noticing patterns in one-shot AI landing pages. Some of them are already popular. Let’s take a look.

**It’s worth mentioning that those are still valid design choices, but the overuse of them is too much when they are not needed.**

### Eyebrows

Overusing eyebrows or pills in the hero section. These usually come before the main title and often include the 🚀 emoji. It might look like a pill, too.

Fresh coffee daily

Roasted Fresh, Delivered Weekly

Small-batch beans roasted to order and shipped within 48 hours. Your best cup starts here.

### Gradient text

Using a gradient for the main headline was already a known style, but overuse nowadays is too much. It usually starts from left to right.

Fresh coffee daily

Roasted Fresh, Delivered Weekly

Small-batch beans roasted to order and shipped within 48 hours. Your best cup starts here.

### Gradient blobs

Oh, I can’t describe how much I dislike this, especially when they are animated.

Fresh coffee daily

Roasted Fresh, Delivered Weekly

Small-batch beans roasted to order and shipped within 48 hours. Your best cup starts here.

### Blur animation effect on headlines

I started to notice this nowadays. In some cases, it happens on the first load, or it might be linked to scrolling. Click on Play to see it in action.

Fresh coffee daily

Roasted Fresh, Delivered Weekly

Small-batch beans roasted to order and shipped within 48 hours. Your best cup starts here.

### Secondary text with lots of NOs

Oftentimes, this “No… No” style uses the word “Fluff”. It’s like having more NOs will change my mind as a user.

Fresh coffee daily

Roasted Fresh, Delivered Weekly

Subscribe today. No fluff. No old beans.

### Cut-off shadows

I see this a lot nowadays, and I don’t know why AI-generated designs share this. Often, when a container has `overflow: hidden` or `overflow: clip`, and its child items have a shadow, the shadow gets cut off.

See it below:

Fresh coffee daily

Roasted Fresh, Delivered Weekly

Small-batch beans roasted to order and shipped within 48 hours. Your best cup starts here.

Usually, the select menu position is stuck to the right side. If I see it, it’s a big sign you didn’t notice or care to fine-tune this.

Fresh coffee daily

Roasted Fresh, Delivered Weekly

Small-batch beans roasted to order and shipped within 48 hours. Your best cup starts here.

And the list goes on. Do you know any other clear signs that a UI is one-shotted?

## A look at some history of web design

### Early web

Websites used to look like an ordered list of links. This was in the late 1990s and very early 2000s. Here is an example of Amazon’s home page.

 ![Amazon homepage in 1999](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/amazon-99-home.BwwzfUOB_Z1pJzvj.png)

You open a website and end up looking at a list of text. That was intentional because of:

-   Slow internet speed: fewer images.
-   Monitor size: show as much info as possible in one view.

In the early 2000s, Apple started using featured images at the top of the page. From there, websites started to follow that pattern.

 ![Apple homepage in 2000](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/apple-2000-home.6n8bj20w_Z2dGmYq.png)

The goal was to show the most important thing we want the user to see first. It might be a new product, an advertisement, a season, a message, you name it. **The point is that there was a goal, not just using it because it was trendy.**

Here is another example of how LEGO looked in 2003.

 ![LEGO homepage in 2003](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/lego-2003-home.CKzdIghI_Zn2CRf.png)

If we analyze these hero designs, we will come up with the following common things:

-   Headline
-   Description or call to action
-   A custom banner design

The goal was to communicate this and promote it to the user as the first thing they see. **If you notice, none of them look the same. Each has its own personality.**

 ![Apple homepage in 2000](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/apple-home-annotated.BKZDDetr_jiUM3.png)

### 2010 - 2020: The responsive design and Bootstrap era

During this time, Bootstrap became so popular that many websites followed the same design language, especially in the hero section.

Here is the popular Bootstrap hero design you’ve probably seen if you designed for the web during that time.

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/bootstrap-hero.DFtwT0Xt_Zez9hW.png)

Why did this happen? Bootstrap gave us the same components and building blocks. If you don’t change the default theme, your site will look like everyone else’s.

Theme marketplaces like ThemeForest also became popular. Template prices were under $50, and you could swap the logo and colors, and it was yours. Back then, 1000s of websites looked the same.

**In other words, lots of websites used the same templated, so they ended up looking the same. This was 10 years ago, so it happened slowly.**

In 2015, lots of people started noticing how similar web design was. Designers often default to a hero section with an image on the left and content on the right, followed by a 3-column layout.

In his article published in Sep 2015, [Owen Williams](https://thenextweb.com/news/zzzzzz) wrote the following:

> Web design has become this predictable equation that usually combines a big hero block with a call to action at the top followed by feature blocks and full-width colors before another call to action button at the bottom.

Again, this was in 2015, when the pace wasn’t as fast as it is now. As far as I remember, nothing changed. Designers kept building the same UIs but with some tweaks here and there.

Here are more articles on the same topic if you’re into some history.

-   [Beyond The Boring: The Hunt For The Web’s Lost Soul](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2015/07/hunt-for-the-webs-lost-soul/)
-   [Every website looks the same, and that’s ok](https://webdesignerdepot.com/every-website-looks-the-same-and-thats-ok/)

### 2021 - Present

Tailwind CSS made it easy for developers to build faster, and this accelerated the idea of building quickly, but it was nothing compared to today’s AI speed.

Tailwind doesn’t force a specific design style; instead, it provides defaults for colors, spacing, typography, borders, and shadows. Most developers used them as-is or, at best, changed colors and a few small details.

Then came TailwindUI and shadcn, where you can import a full hero section or a pricing table and reuse it in your project.

Again, it’s the same story as Bootstrap. Everyone starts from the same building blocks, and the results look similar.

The speed of building with Tailwind made some of us notice the similar designs on the web, but again, this was on a small scale.

Here is a preview of Linear-inspired designs.

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/linear-like-landing-pages.B0awZYHg_2oHONr.png)

Fast forward to 2025-2026, and we saw something different. AI models became better at building generic user interfaces that look “good enough” at first glance.

And guess what AI learned from? The same templates, themes, and UI kits.

## Why everything looks the same

Lots of designers are using AI to design. Some say that design tools (e.g., Figma) are dead. I don’t care about this. What I care about is making a design reflect its brand without looking like a copy of others.

If you can create a design that feels personal and aligned with the brand you’re working on, I will call that a success.

AI was trained on the following, so you can imagine why the results are bad:

-   Templates and UI kits
-   Component libraries (Tailwind UI, shadcn)
-   Copies of SaaS sites (e.g., Linear)
-   Conversion best practices

We can fix this.

## How to bring the personality back

### But why?

Because code is cheap now, lots of people started building products with AI. This caused a huge wave of designs that look the same. If I see your AI-generated product, I won’t even bother reading the details. I’d be more convinced if I could feel the effort that went into the product.

### Looking at the one-shot design

Consider this one-shot design.

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/Kahwa-v0.CD92fabp_Z16DeRS.png)

You might say that I will tweak this until it looks unique, but there is a problem here. If you can’t separate the current feel from what you imagine in your brain, it will be hard to come up with a useful design.

Starting from a design that looks finished will affect your ability to think creatively about the design and what elements/content you should use. By “creative”, I mean the creative process.

To avoid that, I like to start with a plain wireframe/sketch that contains the content I want to design.

### Content first, always

Before thinking about the design, decide what content you need. This can be a wireframe, sketch, or even a written list. Try to write the content yourself, as this will get you into the design flow. You can use AI to write it, but you won’t fully understand what it’s about.

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/kahwa-content-01.hDWPBdmq_Z1SLnbO.png)

Once we have the first content wireframe ready, we can start thinking about the branding and creative part.

### The brand

At this stage, I might ask myself the following questions:

-   If the brand is a character, what will it look and feel like?
-   What does this cafe look like? What vibes do you feel when you step into their place?
-   What does their coffee bag look like? Is it made fully from cardboard, or does it have a custom, aluminum-like feel?

Thinking about those questions will give you hints, and of course there are a lot of questions to ask, but this is just an idea of what to ask/think about.

Here is a photo from the cafe that will give us a sense of it.

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/cafe-scene.DCtf36Qo_Z2ldg9U.jpg)

After that, I considered using a paper-like background with slight opacity, as it will match the vibe of the cafe’s walls.

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/kahwa-bg.B0ygQ-f5_Z1HywRI.png)

### Adding unique ideas

With this, I wanted to add a unique concept for the header and hero. I went to my sketchbook and did some ideas:

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/kahwa-sketch.Bkw7QYyy_1GpWBb.jpg)

The idea is to use one of the following:

-   Coffee-bag-like shape for the hero section
-   Merge the header and hero and make them look like a coffee bag

I went back to Figma and tried the second option. This looks like a good start. Take a look:

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/Kahwa-v2.CqXi8PFd_Z1RgT0N.png)

I kept playing with the design and tried adding a line in the middle to make it more real, then ditched it and just added a ribbon effect at the top.

### The coffee card

Next, I tackled the coffee card.

> Should we showcase the bag’s design in the card? Or should we use a real-life photo from the coffee origin instead?

I settled on using a real-life photo, but since this isn’t a core thing to the user, it should be there as a hint, not the typical card image pattern we know.

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/Kahwa-v3.DVskj6wt_1c16Uf.png)

And I also explored what the menu will look like. All of this design work took less than 1 hour in Figma, but it was well spent because I have a landing page that doesn’t look like a copy of others.

 ![](https://ishadeed.com/_astro/Kahwa-v4.B5OSOpPT_OELSA.png)

### Uniqueness isn’t always good

You might be wondering why we need to make a design look different; doesn’t that hurt the UX when people are used to specific patterns? This is 100% correct, but here we aren’t going too far into inventing a new design pattern. Those are small touches here and there to help differentiate the brand.

Each project has different needs, but navigation, calls to action, and common patterns shouldn’t change just for uniqueness.

However, some design areas should change to bring out the brand’s character, such as aligning the design direction with the brand through typography, imagery, and illustrations.

## Where does AI fit in the process?

I will use it wherever the work is repetitive. For example, setting up a new project boilerplate, installing dependencies, adding data files for all my content, and building the initial basic design.

I don’t like letting the AI write CSS for me. I want to have full control over how and why certain details are made. Since this design isn’t typical, I recommend more control at this stage.

## Outro

I hope we will go back to focusing on designing valuable products and convince the people of using them by building unique marketing pages and avoid the generic ones that the general public know they are not real or scam.
