-   The research uncovered UX issues and identified UX solutions specific to the Accounts area of ecommerce sites
-   Much has changed in account security and sign in, order management and tracking, and order returns since our last large-scale research in 2018

Key Stats

-   4,000+ new hours of Accounts & Self-Service research

Today at Baymard, we’re announcing the launch of our new **updated and expanded** [Accounts & Self-Service](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-accounts-selfservice) UX research.

We **first investigated** UX issues in Accounts & Self-Service in 2018.

In this update, we’ve **retested all the UX issues** observed in 2018, verified all our UX solutions, wrote new guidelines based on newly observed Accounts & Self-Service UX issues, and rewritten the bulk of our verified guidelines for improved usefulness and clarity.

This work has resulted in 57 new and updated Accounts & Self-Service guidelines based on **1,400+ usability issues** observed during testing.

The 57 guidelines are a **key foundation** for ensuring a high-performing Accounts and Self-Service UX.

Additionally, we’ve included **mobile test observations** for the first time.

As a result, this study represents our **most comprehensive findings** on Accounts & Self Service ecommerce UX.

In this article, we’ll highlight **3 high-level insights** from our new Accounts & Self-Service UX [research findings](https://baymard.com/research).

## New Insights for Accounts & Self-Service UX

Compared to 2018, the landscape of Accounts & Self-Service has **changed dramatically,** particularly in 3 areas.

### 1) Account Security and Sign In

![J.Crew desktop account page with masked input typed in the  “Current Password” field.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176797/original/research-media-file-92fd738b49339bec07e8fbae143a717b.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

![J.Crew desktop account page with error message with password requirements below the “New Password” field with masked input.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176798/original/research-media-file-2505bae7066d87b276306f949cf24163.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

At J.Crew, participants updating their passwords were provided no guidance regarding the site’s [password requirements](https://baymard.com/guidelines/676-informing-users-about-password-creation-requirements), and they were unable to [view their input](https://baymard.com/guidelines/665-show-password-feature), further increasing the likelihood of errors (first image). It is only after a password that doesn’t meet the requirements is submitted that the site discloses the requirements (second image).

The standard username and password combination required to create and sign in to an account is quickly being replaced by **passkeys** (e.g., fingerprint, Face ID, or device PIN) and one-time passcodes (OTPs), which are considered by many users to be more convenient — and more secure.

Indeed, **account security** is also top of mind for users — especially when personal information and saved payment methods are involved.

![Walmart desktop “Personal Info” page with “Sign in preferences” on the right, including two-factor authentication option.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176799/original/research-media-file-683f4a14ac449c39760044e2b60fd177.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

_“I use two-factor authentication — it seems just to be standard now to get a code sent to either my phone number or email address. It seems like a lot of websites are doing that now for safety reasons”_, remarked a participant viewing the sign in preferences at [Walmart](https://baymard.com/ux-benchmark/case-studies/walmart). Participants generally appreciated having the option to enable two-factor authentication (2FA).

![Desktop “Sign in Details” section with options “with” or “without” password, and a two-factor authentication banner above.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176800/original/research-media-file-a49495d1d3bcffb5723ca2be65bb8fd7.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

_“Two-factor authentication — not all the time, I’m going to be really honest. It’s kind of site-dependent, but do I use it — especially if it’s a large account or a large website — something I’m doing a lot on. It’s almost always on, but not always”_, explained a participant at [Home Depot](https://baymard.com/ux-benchmark/case-studies/home-depot), noting the two-factor authentication (2FA) banner at the top of the _“Security & Password”_ page.

![Target desktop “Sign in and security” page with password, two-factor authentication set to “Active”, and a passkey option.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176801/original/research-media-file-c4d81b192ba3b00b925ca718a1e69bd7.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

_“I feel good that whoever may or may not have the password will not be able to log in anymore, but to ensure that doesn’t happen again, I would set up 2-factor authentication”_, remarked a participant at [Target](https://baymard.com/ux-benchmark/case-studies/target). After updating her password she took the extra step to enable two-factor authentication (2FA). _“It is enabled. I’m assuming the next time I sign in, I would need to receive a text message or an email code to be able to log in.”_ Offering optional account security features is a low-friction way for sites to allow security-conscious users to opt in without burdening other users who are less risk-averse.

During recent testing, **81% of participants reported** saving credit card details in their account at sites they shop at frequently, and 60% appreciated when two-factor authentication (2FA), which requires confirmation from a user’s verified account email or phone number before being able to sign in, was available to activate.

It’s therefore key that ecommerce sites **move beyond traditional** username and password combinations to support users signing in to their accounts.

Indeed, as observed during testing, strong visible and optional security features such as [two-factor authentication (2FA) and device management](https://baymard.com/guidelines/3072-account-security-features) **build confidence and trust** with users.

However, these features must be **implemented carefully** to avoid becoming a roadblock to users checking out or managing their account details.

![Mobile sign in form with email and password fields highlighted in red and an error message below.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176802/original/research-media-file-564eaa5153aafa268e4e1458e1ca8252.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

![Mobile form to reset password with masked inputs in both the “New Password” and “Confirm New Password” fields.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176803/original/research-media-file-b599ad3f548e3177680b2a9dce44757c.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

_“Oh, okay. Maybe I typed it wrong. Um, it doesn’t like me”_, laughed a participant (iOS) attempting to sign in at [American Eagle](https://baymard.com/ux-benchmark/case-studies/american-eagle-outfitters) (first image). She proceeded to reset the password, but was unable to verify the new password because it was also only displayed as masked (second image). _“That was a little bit of a pain”_, she remarked negatively after resetting the password.

Next, account sign in is viewed by many users as simply a **hurdle to overcome** to proceed with more productive tasks, such as checking out, tracking orders, managing account details, or initiating returns.

However, when account sign in is problematic, users’ **frustration will mount,** as they haven’t even reached the account feature they’re interested in yet.

### 2) Order Management and Tracking

Order management and tracking continue to be **top priorities** for users.

Indeed, order tracking ranked as the **most important self-service feature** in Baymard’s most recent quantitative study ([#GC051](https://feeds.baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/GC051?time_period=March+2026)).

Whether making a one-time purchase or subscribing to recurring shipments (e.g., every 3 weeks, every 4 months), recent testing reveals that users’ expectations regarding self-service order management and tracking features and information have only **intensified** since 2018.

![Desktop order list with canceled items and the top one with product details shown, the rest with ID and date only.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176804/original/research-media-file-e7e7dbe417fceeaaeab937f4665f9567.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

![Desktop order list with details for a whisk including thumbnail, price, color, name, delivery date, and option to buy again.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176805/original/research-media-file-79c4438414ccacdac543eaa653f71eb0.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

_“I often go to my previously ordered items to buy them again, rather than searching the site to try to find them. If I want to make sure I get that same thing again”_, explained a participant at Bloomingdale’s as she scrolled the “Order List” page looking for a previous order containing a whisk. However, she couldn’t easily locate the desired order because only the order number and order date were visible by default (first image). Consequently, she opened 4 orders before stumbling across the whisk order (second image). Hiding basic item and order details in collapsed sections performed as poorly in testing as not providing these details at all (see guidelines [#3218](https://baymard.com/guidelines/3218-displaying-key-item-and-order-details-on-the-order-list-page) and [#925](https://baymard.com/guidelines/925-order-list-item-info)). Indeed, this design places the burden on users to interact with the interface simply to discover the contents of each order.

The “Order List” and “Order Details” pages serve as **critical starting points** for users when they are looking for specific order-related information, and when they want to modify or cancel an order, start a return, review a purchased item, or revisit a purchased item to buy it again.

Across multiple large-scale studies, we’ve observed that users need easy access to certain **[basic information](https://baymard.com/guidelines/925-order-list-item-info)** and actions to effectively identify and manage their orders.

For example, without a clear understanding of order contents and status, and adequate features to identify desired orders on the “Order List” page, users will be **needlessly delayed** in beginning their order-related tasks, which can result in unwanted additional orders and place more strain on customer service.

Likewise, when key details for items on the [“Subscriptions List” page](https://baymard.com/guidelines/3267-subscriptions-list-page-item-details) are missing or unclear, users will be **unable to easily identify** and manage recurring orders.

![Desktop "Order Tracking" overlay with the tracking number highlighted and some shipping info and tracking history displayed.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176806/original/research-media-file-d62aa7436638c418e771ef43ae55063e.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

![Order tracking page with expected delivery date and time and order status progress displayed on the USPS desktop site.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176807/original/research-media-file-cd08bd5ef47de22848b85ff7833c35c7.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

_“Right now it’s in Opa Locka, which is here in Miami. So, it’s arrived there, but it doesn’t give me an estimated delivery date. So that’s a little disappointing. I would hope that that information would be there”_, complained a participant at Bloomingdale’s when he discovered the “Order Tracking” overlay lacked an estimated delivery date (first image). _“Okay, so here it’s saying, the expected delivery date is today, the 23rd, by 9:00 PM.”_ He searched the tracking number at the carrier site and confirmed the expected delivery date and time (second image). _“Yeah, see, it would be helpful if the information from USPS about the expected delivery date would also be included. I’m just not seeing it anywhere. I’m not seeing it anywhere on the website”_, he lamented after he returned to his tracking information at Bloomingdale’s.

When it comes to **order tracking,** for most users the [key details needed to track an order](https://baymard.com/blog/integrate-tracking-info) are the estimated delivery date, the carrier name, and the subevents or stages of the order delivery process.

Some tracking details will be contained in the order shipment notification email, but users who need more detail on order tracking than is in the shipping notification email will visit the **“Order Tracking” page** on the ecommerce site.

Ideally, the destination page on the ecommerce site will contain **all the tracking information** users need, including photo proof of the delivery.

### 3) Order Returns

![Wayfair desktop list of orders with options to track, edit review, and view details (hovered with mouse).](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176808/original/research-media-file-c58a871d16aa6c3ed4b7992f8fb44466.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

![Wayfair desktop “Order Details” overlay on top of orders list with options to track and edit review.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176809/original/research-media-file-3b326be5098bc27c4100b254169240d0.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

![Desktop footer with the return policy link right-clicked and hovering the option to “Open Link in New Tab” in the menu.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176810/original/research-media-file-198a3561c1dcd6b41ce04ef7b9452455.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

_“Let’s see, ‘View Details’. That’s not very clear. I’m looking for where I can start my return. I don’t see it here — the return part. So let me see, I’ll click ‘Return Policy’.”_ This participant at [Wayfair](https://baymard.com/ux-benchmark/case-studies/wayfair) searched for information about returning an item in the order listing (first image) and in the “Order Details” overlay (second image). She then resorted to opening the [return policy link in the footer](https://baymard.com/guidelines/3273-links-to-the-return-policy-shipping-info-and-order-tracking) (third image). Simply removing the _“Start Return”_ button when the return window has closed, as was the case at Wayfair, created needless uncertainty for participants, and often resulted in wasted time and effort.

![Amazon desktop interface for refund method with 3 options with radio buttons and “Refund to Visa” option has no button.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176811/original/research-media-file-6c675d3480fc4d7984704fed3eb97fbe.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

![Amazon desktop interface for refund method and all 4 options has radio buttons for selection.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176812/original/research-media-file-43e659e2759c0e6037a9771caceb95fa.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

_“Um, this is the tricky part — this is where Amazon gets you — so sneaky! They generally don’t give you the option to put it back on your credit card, unless you’re very — there’s not a bubble here, right? If you’re being very quick about it, you’re going to click ‘Amazon account’, blah, blah, and you’re going to click ‘Return’. They still have your money! I actually want my money back. I don’t want to add this to my gift card amount or whatever. So I think this is a bit sneaky on Amazon’s part.”_ This participant at [Amazon](https://baymard.com/ux-benchmark/case-studies/amazon), having been duped by the [“Refund Method” interface](https://baymard.com/guidelines/3414-selecting-a-return-refund-option) in the past, carefully located and clicked the _“Refund to Visa”_ link (first image), which revealed the radio button for her preferred refund option (second image).

While for many sites returns are an undesirable aspect of ecommerce, how sites perform during returns is **critical for end users.**

A high-performing returns experience can, perhaps counterintuitively, become the basis of a **loyal customer relationship.**

On the other hand, a returns experience fraught with confusion, friction, and unnecessary obstacles will cause some users to **avoid shopping** at a site in the future.

Yet during large-scale testing, participants **frequently struggled** to determine an order’s eligibility for returns and to confidently navigate the return flow.

Moreover, returns has perhaps undergone the **largest transformation** since 2018.

Indeed, in 2018 returning an item typically required finding a box, printing a paper return shipping label, repackaging the item, dropping it off with a carrier, and waiting several weeks for a refund.

On the contrary, during recent testing, participants benefited from label-free — and sometimes box-free — **return shipping methods,** as well as [“Return Pickup”](https://baymard.com/guidelines/3276-return-pickup-method).

Additionally, for some participants, **refunds** were initiated as soon as the returned item was scanned during drop-off or pickup.

Overall, users expect a **much more responsive** returns experience with clear, easy-to-locate return details, multiple return options (including at least one free one), and straightforward refund options and confirmation.

## Support Key Customers with High-Performing Accounts UX

![Wayfair mobile ”Manage Payment Options” page with saved Visa card and options to add credit, debit, and Wayfair credit cards.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176815/original/research-media-file-7f6dacd820531ff6482e0d5fb43eab53.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

![Wayfair mobile ”Manage Payment Options” page with saved Visa card selected and the option to “Delete” the card.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/176816/original/research-media-file-4919443e1fa4a5b3c0b7e1a306e9e651.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

_“It looks like it’s only giving me an option to delete it, not actually update it. Okay. Let me go back — maybe I missed something.”_ A participant (iOS) at [Wayfair](https://baymard.com/ux-benchmark/case-studies/wayfair) wanted to update the expiration date on a saved credit card (first image). _“Nope. Looks like that might be the only way. Let me see if maybe there’s a different option on this. Nope. Well, it looks like I will have to delete the debit card and then reenter it.”_ When she tapped to select the card, she discovered she could only delete it (second image). She spent over 2 minutes searching the _“My Account”_ section for a method to [edit the expiration date](https://baymard.com/blog/editing-credit-card-ux-research); she ultimately resorted to deleting the card and re-adding it with the updated expiration date.

Accounts & Self-Service is a **unique aspect** of the ecommerce user experience.

Unlike the other ecommerce themes we’ve researched, Accounts & Self-Service is often not part of the “direct purchase funnel” — but rather comes **post-purchase.**

However, this **isn’t** to indicate that Accounts & Self-Service is less important than the other ecommerce themes.

Returning users — those most likely to use self-service features most often — are **critically important** to a successful ecommerce business.

This means the Accounts & Self-Service UX performance can be just as important as the UX performance of the [Product Page](https://baymard.com/premium/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux), [Homepage, Category Taxonomy & Main Navigation](https://baymard.com/premium/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice), or any other ecommerce areas when it comes to a site’s overall **long-term sales.**

It’s therefore key that users’ experience in the Accounts area of a site is as smooth as possible to support an **ongoing site-user relationship.**
