The problem

## 64% of product pages tested had at least one critical accessibility issue.

This year, **39,976 Shopify and 19,424 WooCommerce product pages** from stores ranked among the world’s top one million websites were scanned. On **37,874** of them, at least one issue in the highest potential-impact category was discovered.

On a product page, that could mean a customer cannot read important information, use a form or select a buying option.

Critical findings are only part of the picture though. **96%** of product pages failed at least one automated accessibility check.

These are not just technical faults. They can stop customers using a product page. In the UK alone, [an estimated £17 billion in online spending does not happen](https://davedavies.dev/blog/black-friday-cyber-monday-sales-advantage/) each year because people find websites difficult or impossible to use. A customer may use a screen reader, enlarge the page or navigate with a keyboard. And when your product page breaks, they generally don't complain or email you. They just leave and buy from somewhere they can actually use _(your accessible competitor, perhaps?)_.

Understanding the headline

## What a critical finding can mean for your customers

Critical is the highest potential-impact rating.

For your customer, a critical finding could make important product information, a form field or option impossible to use. The report identifies the barriers with the **highest potential impact** and shows how common they are across product pages.

64% of the product pages tested contained at least one critical finding.

[Shopify Opens in a new window](https://www.shopify.com/accessibility/vpat-checkout) and [WooCommerce Opens in a new window](https://developer.woocommerce.com/2025/07/03/a-year-of-accessibility-improvements-in-woocommerce-core/) have both invested significantly in accessibility, particularly in checkout and the parts of the buying journey they directly control.

However, your product page is built from platform code, a theme, apps and plugins, agency or developer work and the store's own content.

**Any of those layers can introduce a barrier.**

That's why accessibility needs an owner inside the business. Someone needs to find where a problem sits, assign it to the right team or supplier and make sure it gets fixed.

Overlays

## Accessibility overlays fix... nothing.

Accessibility overlays are often sold as a quick way to make a website accessible. The appeal is obvious: one product promises to take a complicated problem off your hands. However...

62% of the **1,691** product pages where an accessibility overlay widget was detected still had **at least one critical accessibility failure.**

Installing an overlay is not proof that the accessibility work is done.

An accessibility overlay is software added to a website, often visible as a floating accessibility button, which promises to repair problems automatically. The [Overlay Fact Sheet Opens in a new window](https://overlayfactsheet.com/en/) sets out the accessibility community's wider position on these products, as well as the legal risk.

If your store uses one, the next step is straightforward: test the product page itself and fix what customers still encounter.

The same page structure that helps your customers shop also helps search engines and machines understand your products too.

Clear headings, useful [image descriptions Opens in a new window](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) and properly built, labelled form fields help your customers find product information, navigate by keyboard or shop with a screen reader. Those same elements also tell search crawlers and AI assistants what the product is, which options are available and what each button, link and field does.

Shoppers can use AI assistants to compare products before they visit a store, and increasingly AI providers are starting to allow purchases directly through their platforms. Clear descriptions, current prices and stock information give those services less to guess at.

Method

## How the research was carried out

The research covers 59,400 product pages, all tested using one consistent process.

One product page was selected per Shopify or WooCommerce store in HTTP Archive's global top-million website dataset. Every page included in the report received the same mobile Lighthouse accessibility audit through Google PageSpeed Insights.

The full methodology explains how stores and product pages were selected, checked and counted.
