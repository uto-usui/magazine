_A journey through the messiest, most rewarding research challenges that taught me everything I know about making the impossible inevitable_

Picture this: It’s 2 PM on a Tuesday, and I’m staring at a survey dashboard that’s basically mocking me. The response rate was low, as expected, but the data quality was questionable at best. My carefully crafted mobile survey, designed for Uber drivers in India, was failing spectacularly.

But here’s the thing — I wasn’t just dealing with low response rates. I was dealing with responses that made no sense. Drivers were selecting contradictory answers, abandoning surveys halfway through, and when they did complete them, there were predictable patterns like filling same option for all questions. It was like trying to understand someone’s life story through a game of telephone played in three different languages.

And this wasn’t just one project. This was my reality, project after project. Traditional recruitment? Even with an incredible research ops team backing me, it felt like I was constantly hitting the same wall, drivers were unresponsive, and when they did respond, they rarely showed up for the interviews.

That’s when it hit me: I wasn’t solving the right problem. I was so focused on perfecting my research methods that I’d forgotten the most important question — how do people actually live and work in the real world?

### When your users are always moving (literally)

Working with Uber drivers in India taught me my first hard lesson about operational thinking. These weren’t people with the luxury of sitting down for a 30-minute user interview over Zoom. These were folks working 12–14 hour days, where every minute away from the road meant lost income for their families.

Traditional recruitment? Forget about it. Even with an amazing research ops team (seriously, they were incredible), recruiting drivers for studies felt like trying to find a needle in a haystack that keeps moving.

But here’s where it gets interesting — and where I learned my most valuable lesson.

### Finding gold in the operations team

I started hanging out with the city operations teams, initially, not for research purposes, but because I was genuinely curious about how they managed to coordinate with thousands of drivers every day. What I discovered was beautiful in its simplicity: they had something I desperately needed — trust, regular touchpoints, and most importantly, they were solving real problems that drivers cared about.

So I did something that felt a little crazy at the time: I asked if I could embed research into their existing workflows.

**What started as one experiment became my entire research strategy.**

That first mobile survey disaster? We turned it into an in-person conversation guide that ops team members could use during their regular driver interactions. As a result, we obtained more reliable survey responses across several cities. Additionally, the research project helped the local operations team improve their conversational and assessment abilities with driver partners. This project became a great way to build connections.

After this big win, I recognised the immense value in partnering with local operations teams. Vehicle inspections became opportunities to understand driver pain points. Onboarding sessions turned into research moments about new driver experiences. Hub visits became opportunities to delve into earnings concerns.

All of these became recruitment goldmines for different research studies, and the time to recruit was negligible because there were hundreds of drivers coming to these centres, and all I needed to do was show up with my plan, guide, and spot recruit.

But here’s the key part: I didn’t just hand over questions and walk away. I became a collaborator with the ops team. Whenever we had a project — which was every two weeks — I’d spend time with ops team leads, helping them understand the focus of the project and who I was looking to recruit. Whether it was qualitative or quantitative work, I’d collaborate with them on the plan to scale and get responses. I’d listen to their feedback as they had so much context and cultural nuances I was completely missing.

Project after project, this approach kept delivering:

-   **Consistent and reliable response rates** (Recruitment & Administration)
-   **Rich, contextual insights that actually made sense** (Scope)
-   **Research timelines were cut in half because we weren’t fighting recruitment battles** (Stakeholder Engagement & Communication)

And the best part? Each project made the next easier. The ops team began proactively identifying opportunities and bringing observations to me.

What began as solving one “impossible” survey became a systematic, scalable way to conduct research with a population widely recognised as difficult to recruit.

### What ResearchOps teams can take away from this

-   **Embed recruitment into existing operational workflows** by partnering with frontline teams who already have trusted, regular contact with participants.
-   **Collaborate early and often** with operational leads to align recruitment plans with real-world contexts and cultural nuances.
-   **Leverage operational touchpoints as research moments** to build trust and surface richer insights.
-   **Measure success beyond numbers:** prioritize response quality, timeline efficiency, and stakeholder buy-in, not just recruitment volume.

By thinking like an operator, not just a researcher, I transformed “impossible” recruitment challenges into repeatable, impactful systems. If you’re facing similar walls, take a step back and look for the operational pathways already in place — you might find your research highway where you least expect it.
