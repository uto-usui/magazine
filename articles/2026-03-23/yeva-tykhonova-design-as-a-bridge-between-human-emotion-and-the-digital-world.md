Hi, I’m Eva, a multidisciplinary designer with a background in code and a deep interest in the emotional side of digital experiences.

I believe high emotional intelligence and sensitivity are among the most important qualities of a designer. Design is the ability to evoke the right feelings in users, create emotional contact, and guide interaction with a product or brand, all while making it work for the business. For me, that is what design really is.

In a way, this is also why writing this article felt challenging. I didn’t want it to be just a collection of projects or a simple presentation of my work. I wanted it to carry something more, a feeling.

If this article can inspire someone, make them pause for a moment, or reflect on what design means to them and how it shapes their everyday experiences, then it has already done its job.

## **A Little About My Journey**

My love for design was obvious from childhood. I was drawn to different forms of it, especially interior and fashion design, and spent a lot of time exploring those worlds.

However, I started in the digital industry with development. At the time, coding felt clear and structured, and honestly, I also believed I wasn’t talented enough to become a designer.

But while working on development for other designers, something slowly changed. The more projects I built, the more I realized I wanted to express ideas through design, not just implement them.

Despite fear and doubt, I decided to start learning design. At first, it was almost a challenge to myself, to prove that I could do it. But within a year, design in all its forms became a true passion and an essential part of my life. Today, I can’t imagine my life without it.

I’d like to show the projects that have become milestones in my journey, work that reflects my passion and the inspiring people behind it.

## Projects Highlights

### [Inna Dove Studio](https://innadove.com/en/main-en/)

#### Brand Identity

The project began with creating the brand identity. The goal was to develop a visual language that would reflect the studio’s philosophy and aesthetic approach to space.

The identity focuses on elegance, balance, and restraint. Through refined typography, minimal forms, and a calm visual rhythm, the brand communicates a sense of sophistication and timelessness. The intention was to create an identity that feels quiet yet confident, allowing the studio’s work and attention to detail to remain at the center.

#### Web Design

The goal was to translate the studio’s aesthetic and philosophy into a digital experience. Interior design is deeply emotional because it shapes how people live, feel, and experience space, and it was important that the website reflected that sensitivity.

The design focuses on calm composition, generous space, and a clear visual rhythm that allows the interiors to speak for themselves. Rather than competing with the projects, the interface quietly supports them and creates a digital environment that feels elegant, immersive, and natural to explore.

## [EMMPO](https://emmpo.com/)

EMMPO is one of my favorite projects. The goal was to design a bold and vibrant website that would attract a Gen Z audience, especially young girls, and invite them to explore their future through a career test.

I was responsible for the full visual direction of the project, including the branding, visual identity, and website design. The challenge was to create an experience that felt energetic, expressive, and visually memorable while still being clear and easy to navigate.

This project received several industry recognitions, including **Site of the Day on Awwwards**, **GSAP** **Site of the Day**, a **Business & Services Honors on Awwwards**, and a **Muzli Pick**.

## Brutalist UI Concept for a Perfume Store

This concept project was inspired by the work of the photographer Cho Ge Seok, whose visual language and artistic world I deeply admire. I’m inspired by the way he expresses himself through photography and the way he sees and feels the world.

While exploring his visual universe, I wanted to translate that atmosphere into a digital experience through design. Brutalist aesthetics felt like the perfect visual language for this exploration. Strong typography, high contrast, and bold composition create a raw and expressive interface that reflects the intensity and attitude present in his work.

For me, this project became an experiment in translating a creative world into digital form, trying to capture a certain mood and energy through design.

## Visual Experiments I Can’t Stop Loving

Besides client projects, I love creating design just for myself, to experiment, explore new ideas, and simply enjoy the process. These experiments allow me to express my creativity, bring to life ideas that pop into my head, and create a space for free, emotional expression.

They also inspire me in my work and often spark inspiration in others, showing how design can be playful, personal, and emotionally engaging.

## **A little bit (or maybe not) about my Design Philosophy**

Design is described as problem-solving, and while that is true, I believe it is only part of the story. Design is also about creating a feeling.

For me, design becomes a bridge connecting human experiences through technology.

We often say that design should be clear, functional, and user-friendly. And it absolutely should. But interaction is always emotional. In a world where new technologies and AI tools appear almost every day, it is easy to forget something fundamental:

**Behind every interface, there are real people. Yet in the digital world, it’s easy to forget that a “user” is not just a persona in a brief or a statistic in analytics.**

It is a real person, someone with needs, emotions, attention, and limited time. Every interaction begins with a feeling. Every decision to explore, trust, engage, or leave starts with an emotional response. Because of that, when I design digital experiences, I think not only about layouts and systems.

Good design works on two levels at once:

-   structural clarity (functional foundation, hierarchy, navigation, rhythm, and logic);
-   emotional impact.

When these two layers support each other, design becomes strong, not just technical accuracy, but a living, emotional point of contact.

## **Why Creativity Matters**

Creativity is another essential aspect of my design approach. But for me, creativity isn’t just about self-expression or visual tricks, it’s about taking a brand to a new level of visibility and connection with its audience.

When a clear structure meets a personal creative layer, design becomes more than a solution. It is about translating something that exists internally, a feeling, intuition, or observation, into form, color, rhythm, or pause within a layout.

Real creativity often begins when you stop trying to “invent something clever” and instead start listening to what the project actually wants to express.

## **Inspired by the World, Driven by People**

Of course, like any designer, I’m inspired by everything around me, art, music, film, fashion, architecture, and all forms of design. Every visual, every story, every experience sparks ideas and shapes the way I see the world.

But the greatest source of inspiration for me is people, their stories, ideas, and the worlds they want to bring to life through their businesses.

Helping them realize their vision through design, translating their passion, mission, and personality into visual and interactive experiences, is what truly drives me.

## **In the End, It’s About Feeling**

Technology will continue to evolve, tools will change, the web will keep transforming, but the essence of design will remain the same.

We are **people creating for people.**

And the most meaningful work happens when structure, creativity, and human understanding meet in the same place. Because in the end, design is not only about how something looks or works, it is about how it makes someone feel.
