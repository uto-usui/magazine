## UXPA Survey Results

-   **The data were collected from June 2026–September 2026** using a [non-probability](https://measuringu.com/online-panels/) sample. Initial respondents were recruited through UXPA membership and postings on professional networks such as LinkedIn. Additional respondents were recruited using [snowball sampling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowball_sampling). Keep this sampling approach in mind as it impacts the generalizability of the findings and comparability to earlier data.

-   **International reach, most from the U.S.** The survey collected 383 responses from 24 countries, with 82% coming from the U.S. Other significant numbers came from Canada (5%), the UK (4%), and Germany (2%). The sample size was the smallest we’ve ever collected for this survey, though only a little smaller than the 2024 sample (_n_ = 444).

-   **The median salary was $143K.** This 19% increase is significantly higher than the 2024 median of $120K. While that sounds great, there are two factors that make this result more sobering. First, the [inflation rate from 2024 to 2026](https://www.bls.gov/data/inflation_calculator.htm) **was 6.3%**, reducing the inflation-adjusted increase to about 12%. Second, we observed **a shift in the composition of our 2026 sample**, which inflated the overall median salary—specifically:
    
    -   The **percentage of responses from the United States _increased 15 points_** from 67% in 2024 to 82% in 2026. Historically, salaries in the U.S. are at least 50% higher than non-U.S.
    -   The **percentage of respondents with fewer than five years of UX experience _decreased 16 points_** from 29% in 2024 to 13% in 2026.
    -   Consistent with the lower experience level, the **percentage of lower-level job titles (entry and mid-level non-supervisory) _decreased 8 points_** from 32% in 2024 to 24% in 2026.

-   **Many variables affect salary.** Because the $143K average for 2026 is based on many variables, you can’t look at that alone to gauge how well your own salary stacks up. Variables that can affect salary levels include location (country with regional effects within the U.S.), time working in UX, educational degree (e.g., bachelor’s vs. doctorate), employment level (entry vs. mid and senior level positions), and company size.

**Table 2:** Key drivers from the UXPA 2026 survey explain 73% of the variation in salaries.

The regression model accounted for 73% of the variation in salaries. For the behavioral sciences, explaining this much variation is excellent. Individual factors—skills, personalities, negotiation, company policies, etc.—also affect your salary; keep that in mind as you review the numbers and use the calculator below.

As in years past, the most influential factors for salaries in the UX field are:

1.  **Where you live:** The country (and region for the U.S.) has the biggest impact on what you’re paid. It alone explains 50% of the variation in salary data. The 2026 median salary in the U.S. is $153K, compared to $100K in the UK, $81K in Canada, and $68K in Germany.

In the U.S., the highest regional salaries are in Northern California ($180K), the Northeast ($165K), and the Pacific Northwest ($161K). The lowest regional salaries were in the Midwest ($133.5K), Southern California ($137K), and the Southeast ($148K).

The map below (Figure 1) shows the number of usable salaries we analyzed for each state and how we labeled the regions. The patterns in 2026 were generally the same as those in 2024.

2.  **Job level:** The next biggest driver of salaries is job level, accounting for 11% of the variation in salaries. On average, senior-level supervisors make over twice the salary of entry-level employees ($180K vs. $68K).

3.  **Experience:** Accounting for 4% of the variation in salary, the more experience you have, the more you get paid. In a way, this percentage is deceptively low because, in the UXPA regression model, experience can range from 0 to more than 25 years while other predictors in the model are categorical.

4.  **Company size/type:** The bigger the company, generally the bigger the paycheck, and in 2026 this variable accounted for 2% of the variation in salaries. It’s hard for small companies to compete with the big payroll of larger companies. Employees at companies with fewer than 1,000 employees had a median of $125K compared to employees at larger firms (over 1,000 employees) who make on average $153K.

5.  **Education:** Your degree matters (at least a little). Around 13% of respondents reported having a Ph.D. Respondents with a Ph.D. make on average $154K compared to those with master’s degrees ($150K) or bachelor’s degrees ($135K). These differences in degrees accounted for 2% of the variation in salary data.

6.  **Age (Not Significant):** As in 2024, with job levels and years of experience in the model (which have high correlation with age), age itself contributed no additional measurable effect on salary in 2026.

7.  **Gender (Not Significant):** This year, as in years past, gender was not a significant predictor in the UXPA regression model. As is typical historically, the median salary for male respondents in 2026 ($150K) was higher than that for female respondents ($140K), with the difference well within the margin of error.
