The overall UX performance of **the average** sports gear site was “mediocre,” leaning towards “decent”.

Thus, **no sites** delivered a “good” or better overall user experience.

These were among the most **common issues** encountered in the benchmark:

Key **breakdowns** appeared in areas like filter coverage, where activity-specific or seasonal refinements were often missing; product galleries, which lacked tools like zoom or scale references; and specification content, which was inconsistently structured or overly dense.

Search often returned relevant products, but **rarely offered** the guidance needed to explore by sport or use case.

At checkout, missing cost estimates and limited shipping visibility **disrupted** purchase decisions.

Post-purchase, weak account dashboards and inconsistent confirmation logic left users with **little support** for tracking, updating, or repeating orders.

Together, these **UX gaps** made it harder to navigate product complexity, compare confidently, or complete common ecommerce tasks efficiently.
