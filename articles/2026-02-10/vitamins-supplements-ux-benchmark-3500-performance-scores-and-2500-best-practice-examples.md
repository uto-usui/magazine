The overall UX performance of **the average** vitamins and supplements site was “mediocre”, leaning towards “poor”, with 4 sites in the “mediocre” and 3 in the “poor” range.

Thus, **no sites** delivered a “decent” or better overall user experience ​​— clearly, there’s much room for improvement.

These were among the most **common issues** encountered in the benchmark:

The vitamins and supplements sites often **failed** to support the complex, health-driven needs of their audience.

Many product pages **lacked structured layouts**, leaving users to sift through dense information when comparing ingredients or benefits.

Cart and checkout sections were **missing** key order details, and few provided reassurance around shipping timing or totals.

Search tools handled exact queries capably but **broke down** when users searched by symptom, health goal, or dietary need — common entry points in this category.

Finally, account tools were inconsistent and often underdeveloped, generally **lacking** the polish and structure needed to support confident, efficient self-service.
