_How shifting from projects to programs transformed my approach to recruiting B2B users_

Try scheduling a 60-minute interview with a small business owner. Go ahead, I’ll wait.

Ask any researcher working on B2B products — the challenge of recruitment is real. It only gets tougher as business size increases. These people juggle payroll, customer complaints, supply chain issues, and marketing campaigns — often all before lunch. Their time isn’t just valuable; it’s their entire livelihood.

Sometimes, the incentives we offer for participation are less than what their time is worth. They’re eager to give feedback, but scheduling is a major challenge. And unlike typical B2C products, sheer participant numbers just don’t exist in B2B.

Yet understanding their needs is crucial for building products that truly meet their needs.

## The program thinking breakthrough — Research is a product

This is where I had my major operational revelation: I needed to stop thinking like a researcher running isolated projects and start thinking like someone building a **product** — with research itself as the product.

I built what I call a **research operating system**. Instead of recruiting for single studies, I focused on building ongoing relationships and creating a panel. I reached out to business owners consistently — not just when I had a study — but because I genuinely wanted to understand their world better.

I made participating in research valuable for them, not just for us, by creating meaningful opportunities to connect. I always offered multiple ways to participate in research, ensured flexibility in participation, made sure participants knew what to expect, and provided any support they needed. This made recruitment feel personal and respectful of their busy lives.

I also created a knowledge repository at the back of this program, so every research intervention done with the business was documented and recorded. This helped avoid redundancies in research efforts, helped make the qualification of participants easier and most importantly, also prevented us from asking businesses the same questions again.

## Planning for real lives and real business cycles

People have lives. Small business owners go on vacation. They have intense work seasons and quieter periods. I planned for this varying engagement from the start, so owners could participate based on availability — and I could plan research activities based on panel capacity.

The panel wasn’t a static list checked off once complete. It was a **continuous process**, aiming to keep active participation at sustainable levels.

Typically, panels have recruitment and management. So, once the participant is on the panel, they commit to multiple research cycles. This normally requires continuous engagement, and for businesses, it means engaging in yet another platform. To keep the panel going, meaningful engagement becomes critical, which increases the amount of work on the research team.

**The magic moment**

The magic happened when these business owners started reaching out to me. “Hey, we just tried this new feature — thought you’d want to know how it’s working.” That’s when I knew I’d built something sustainable.

I created what I called a **Rolling Research Program** — a program that breathes with the natural rhythms of business cycles. We’d ramp up research during slower periods, scale back during busy seasons, and stay connected with lighter-touch methods in between.

## Integrating research into business relationships

What really made it work was stopping the mindset of research as a separate entity. Sales calls, customer success check-ins, account management meetings — they all became **research opportunities** because I trained teams to recognise and capture insights naturally in conversations.

## The “Aha” moments that changed my approach

**Working with systems, not against them  
**Every “impossible” research challenge already had a solution — I just wasn’t looking in the right place. Ops teams weren’t barriers; they were highways to better research. Sales calls, Customer Support interactions and marketing touchpoints were goldmines to be leveraged to unblock operational challenges for my research.

**Relationships > transactions  
**Traditional research often feels transactional: insights in exchange for time and payment. The best insights come from genuine relationships built over time. When people trust you and know you’re invested in understanding their world — not just extracting data — the quality of insights skyrockets.

## What ResearchOps teams can take away from this

-   **Think of research as a product** that requires continuous development, nurturing, and user engagement (Program Management).
-   **Build rolling panels and ongoing relationships** instead of one-off participant lists (Recruitment & Administration).
-   **Create flexible participation options** respecting users’ time and business cycles.
-   **Train cross-functional teams to spot and capture research opportunities** in everyday conversations (Stakeholder Engagement & Communication).
-   **Prioritise trust and relationship-building** over transactional incentives.

It’s crucial to stop fighting the realities of their users’ busy lives and instead **embrace operational thinking** — building systems that work _with_ users and business rhythms, not against them.

By treating research as a product and participants as partners, we unlock insights that are not just useful but transformative.

Because at the end of the day, sustainable research programs don’t just gather data — they build lasting understanding.
