The **overall UX** performance of the Mass Merchant sites ranges from “poor” to “good”, with most sites in the “mediocre” to “decent” range.

Here are some of the **overarching UX issues**.

The industry performance highlights **user experience challenges** across the industry, particularly regarding [product discoverability](https://baymard.com/blog/findability-vs-discoverability-ux), cost transparency, and the consistency of product data.

While homepages presented the main navigation categories and a broad range of product types, [**ads occupied prime positions**](https://baymard.com/guidelines/240-ad-looking-content-on-the-homepage), and intermediary category pages gave [no route to a full product list](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2178-viewing-all-products-on-intermediary-category-pages).

Search **often struggled** with [synonyms](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2718-handling-synonyms), [misspellings](https://baymard.com/guidelines/355-handling-misspellings-on-search-results-pages), and [sorting for search results](https://baymard.com/guidelines/393-sorting-logic-for-sitewide-search-results) that disregarded relevance.

**Product lists omitted** [unit prices](https://baymard.com/premium/research-articles/price-per-unit), [stock status](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2369-showing-that-items-are-out-of-stock), and the attributes that [distinguish similar items](https://baymard.com/guidelines/443-consistency-of-product-attributes-across-list-items-and-search-results), which made product evaluation more difficult.

Furthermore, order cost and delivery timing stayed **unstated** until late in the flow.

Specifically, multiple product page “Buy” sections **provided no** estimate of the [order total](https://baymard.com/guidelines/825-providing-a-total-order-cost) or a [date it would arrive](https://baymard.com/guidelines/802-where-and-how-to-display-delivery-information), and in checkout, shipping options were frequently labeled by [speed rather than by a delivery date](https://baymard.com/premium/research-articles/shipping-speed-vs-delivery-date).

These **friction points**, combined with address entry that was [largely unaided](https://baymard.com/research-articles/automatic-address-lookup) and account menus whose [paths were incomplete](https://baymard.com/guidelines/876-what-to-include-in-the-account-menu) and [ambiguously named](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2973-ambiguous-paths-in-the-account-menu), created a shopping environment that hindered product comparison and [efficient checkout](https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-flow-ux-optimization).

In summary, with **only 1 site** delivering a “good” overall user experience, there is plenty of room for improvement.
