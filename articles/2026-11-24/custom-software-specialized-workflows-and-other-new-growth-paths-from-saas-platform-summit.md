AI is making it easier for SaaS platforms to build tools for merchant needs that were previously considered too niche to justify the cost of development. A freight broker, for instance, had to use a mechanical equipment manufacturer’s online portal to compete for shipping jobs. It was a slow, manual process. So, [Goodlane Logistics](https://goodlanelogistics.com/) spent two hours building an interface that automates the bidding process—for one person. Goodlane’s customer success teams now also build custom software for each merchant’s quarterly business review, turning CSV exports and spreadsheets into interactive dashboards within Goodlane’s platform.

“That would have been crazy five years ago,” said Sam Gerstenzang, cofounder of startup incubator [Boulton & Watt](https://boultonwatt.com/), who joined John Collison, cofounder of Stripe, for a [fireside conversation](https://go.stripe.global/Summit-2026-on-demand#fireside-chat) at Stripe’s SaaS Platform Summit earlier this month. “You’d have thought it’s not scalable, but \[with AI\], we’re now building custom software that can have a huge impact for each customer.”

Platforms have always offered software tailored to vertical-specific workflows. AI now makes it possible for them to build software that’s also merchant-specific. At Stripe’s SaaS Platform Summit, nearly 200 founders, operators, and payments leaders discussed how solving more specialized merchant problems can open new revenue streams.

[Jane](https://jane.app/), for example, helps health and wellness practices manage patient bookings, clinical charting, insurance billing, and payments. Legal AI platform [Clio](https://www.clio.com/) offers financing to help law firms cover payroll while waiting for clients to pay. [Squarespace](https://stripe.com/customers/squarespace) gives financial accounts to small businesses building on its platform, and Instant Payouts helps them access their earnings faster. 

Three insights stood out at the SaaS Platform Summit.

## 1\. AI can clear technical benchmarks and still fall short on trust

AI is changing what platforms can afford to build, “but that doesn’t mean it’s easier to make good decisions on what to build,” said A.J. Axelrod, who leads payments and financial services at Clio. “As the barriers to building fall, the ability to build the right things becomes an even greater competitive advantage. That requires a deep understanding of your customers, the challenges they face, and what will make a meaningful difference in their lives.” That includes understanding what they’ll trust AI to do.

For example, [Moxie](https://www.joinmoxie.com/) helps nurses and doctors open and run med spas. When it introduced voice AI to handle med spa calls and booking prospective clients, it found that customers were less forgiving of mistakes made by the AI than those made by the human team. “The voice version needed to be perfect for the people to trust it,” Gerstenzang said. “That experience revealed a different customer acceptance threshold, not just a technical benchmark to clear.”

Platforms adding AI products also have to decide how to charge for the value those products provide, while showing merchants how those charges reflect the work AI does for them. Scott Woody, CEO of [Metronome](https://metronome.com/), a Stripe product, described how companies like [HubSpot](https://go.stripe.global/ai-agent-monetization-amer) combine subscriptions with usage-based AI charges—keeping its seat-based pricing in place. “Your job as a platform is to make sure that users have full visibility into their spend, predictability over how much they’re going to spend, and controls to make sure they don’t overspend,” said Woody.

## 2\. Platforms are taking on the harder parts of running a business with embedded finance

Platforms are embedding financial services into their software and taking on more of merchants’ daily work, including payroll, bookkeeping, and marketing—tasks merchants once handled themselves or outsourced to separate providers. In a survey of SaaS companies that started with a nonfinancial product and went on to add a second product, [45%](https://assets.stripeassets.com/fzn2n1nzq965/4H9Pi41AGQoFNGLt9BMaRn/efd4c60d559208372925b380e37d30d6/Vertical_And_SMB_SaaS_Benchmark_Report_2025.pdf) chose to offer fintech.

Merchant behavior can identify financial work businesses need help managing. For example, Jane saw its Canadian clinics tracking hours in spreadsheets and using e-transfers to pay salaried staff and independent contractors. The company embedded payroll and built support for time tracking and variable pay rates, bringing more of the operational work into one place. “Nobody is going to ask you for a financial account or embedded bookkeeping,” said Emily Bartels, VP of payments and financial services at Jane. “You have to be good at listening for where the pain starts to show up.” 

Moxie works backward from profit and loss statements to find where med spa owners spend their time and money, then decides which problems it’s best positioned to solve. The platform now provides marketing, payments, and purchasing. Gerstenzang wants to take on more operational and risk work that med spa owners would otherwise manage themselves—from forming the LLC and navigating licensing and HIPAA requirements to placing bulk orders for pharmaceuticals and Botox. “We literally want there to be three expenses for that \[med spa\] business,” he said. “Taxes, real estate, and Moxie.”

## 3\. AI tools help platforms make faster pricing decisions, even as they add products

As platforms add products and pricing models, it gets harder to see where growth is translating into revenue and where margins are slipping. AI tools help teams pinpoint which merchants or changes in payment mix are driving those shifts, and decide what to change much more quickly.

During the summit, Stripe announced expanded capabilities for [Stripe Console](https://docs.stripe.com/dashboard/console), an agentic payments expert for platforms. Console helps platforms understand payments performance, find revenue opportunities, and solve margin issues. A Console demo at the event illustrated how larger transactions at a single merchant could lower a platform’s [take rate](https://youtu.be/RLwtsHktVo8?t=342), even as its overall payments volume grew. To fix the issue, Console was able to recommend a specific pricing strategy benchmarked against similar companies on Stripe’s network of 20,000 platforms and 12 million businesses.

The example investigation took minutes and didn’t require anyone to pull reports and compare accounts manually.
