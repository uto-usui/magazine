Welcome to Source Material, a weekly series where the designers and creatives shaping culture share the references that shaped them. Figma’s first guest is Thom Bettridge, editor in chief of [i-D Magazine](https://i-d.co/) and founder of [Content World](https://www.content.world/).

After the shoot, we caught up with Thom about his creative process:

**What were you making when you realized, “This could be my job”?**

My first issue working at [032c](https://www.032c.com/), which was the Raf Simons retrospective issue. I remember getting that one back from the printer and thinking, “Damn, I helped make this…and it’s really fucking good.”

**What’s the first thing you do when you sit down to work?**

I can’t do anything resembling work without a) music, and b) Yerba Madre.

**What’s a design object you’d take to your grave?**

Probably my teddy bear. But it would also be cool to have a [Barcelona couch](https://www.knoll.com/shop/en_us/living-benches-ottomans/barcelona-couch/2144.html) on top of my grave, so people can lie with me for a bit.

**Whose work makes you jealous?**

Stanley Kubrick. And George Lois.

**What current design trend are you tired of seeing?**

To be honest, all of them. If anything you’re doing is a “trend,” self-assess and change your game up.

**What rule do you break the most?**

I like it when text disrupts, blocks, or corrupts an image. We’ve become so precious with images as an industry—probably because it’s so expensive to produce a fashion photograph—that there’s this unwritten rule that graphic design should get out of an image’s way. I love breaking that rule.

**What song do you put on to get out of a creative rut?**

[“1 Thing” by Amerie](https://open.spotify.com/track/1mnqraQ8oV8MX92rdOFLWW?si=5d23969de8b84663)

**What’s a piece of feedback you resisted, and later realized was right?**

I’ve always been drawn to visual chaos—which I think reflects the inherent turbulence of the world today. But some people don’t like it, and even I’ve learned to mellow out a bit.

**What’s a part of your process that most people don’t know about?**

I think people are sometimes surprised that I have zero skill at any craft—I can’t draw, take a photograph, design a page. To be honest, I barely know how to use my laptop. But creativity is a team sport, and I’ve had the benefit of working with enormously talented people.

**What’s the last thing you came across that made you want to go make something?**

A gnome in a forest.
