The **overall UX** performance of the B2B Electronic Components & Machinery sites ranges from “poor” to “decent”, with most sites performing poorly and only one site within the “decent” range.

Here are just a few of the **overarching UX issues** we observed.

The B2B Electronic Components & Machinery sites largely **fell short of** the UX standards the procurement context demanded.

[Compatible parts](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2667-showcasing-compatible-parts), [substitutes](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2659-providing-verified-substitute-products), and [alternatives](https://baymard.com/guidelines/810-alternative-product-cross-sells) were almost entirely **unsupported** across the industry.

In a domain where out-of-stock and end-of-life parts were routine, the inability to reach validated alternatives from the product page left a **significant sourcing gap**.

B2B ecommerce signals were **frequently missing** at the point where purchasing decisions needed to be made.

Minimum order quantities and volume price breaks (see guideline [#2432](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2432-minimum-order-quantity-moq-and-price-breaks-in-the-product-table-item-details)), [shipping timeframes](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2420-shipping-timeframe-for-in-stock-items), and in-stock quantity were **inconsistently surfaced** across both the [Product Table](https://baymard.com/research-articles/use-product-tables-for-desktop-product-listings) and the [buy section](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2617-placement-of-inventory-and-shipping-details).

In a procurement context defined by fixed budgets and project deadlines, their absence left the most **critical purchasing inputs unavailable** at the point where decisions needed to be made.

Cart information and B2B-specific functionality **fell short** of procurement expectations

Around a third of sites fail to provide key B2B [shipping and delivery details](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2694-shipping-and-delivery-details-in-the-cart) in the cart, **omitting information** directly relevant to procurement deadlines at the first point where order costs are evaluated.

More than half of the sites **provide no** [“Share,” “Download,” or “Save” features](https://baymard.com/guidelines/2682-cart-collaboration-features-for-b2b-items) for cart contents.

In a B2B context where orders frequently require internal review or approval before purchase, the absence of these features **forces reliance on workarounds** such as screenshots or manual transcription.

In summary, across the moments that mattered most in the procurement workflow, evaluation, comparison, and ordering, the sites provided **limited support**.

As this benchmark shows, with **only 1 site** delivering a “decent” overall user experience, there is plenty of room for improvement.
