Key Stats & Takeaways

-   20+ new insights on the influence of AI on shopper habits and preferences
-   2,273 US online shoppers surveyed in this quantitative UX study
-   While shoppers are rapidly adopting AI for product discovery, critical hesitation remains around accuracy, imagery, and purchasing authority, making transparent design and easy fact-checking essential for retailers

We’ve released new [Quantitative Insights](https://baymard.com/blog/quantitative-insights-launch) into the perceptions and usage of AI tools in ecommerce, adding to our growing body of data on the habits and preferences of online shoppers.

These insights are **survey-based data visualizations** that complement and deepen our large-scale [UX research findings](https://baymard.com/research) of shoppers’ AI engagement.

The 20+ insights address the use of AI in the **online shopping experience**, spanning a breadth of topics: product research entry points, AI feature usage and interest, trust and privacy concerns, perceived accuracy of AI content, confidence in AI-generated images, AI purchasing authority limits, preferences for AI vs human support, and responsibility for AI purchasing errors.

Online shoppers navigate a **delicate balance of convenience and trust** when encountering AI across their online shopping journey.

While shoppers **eagerly adopt AI** for fast product discovery and feature comparisons, they remain skeptical when evaluating AI-generated product images or resolving order issues.

Beyond early-stage product research, consumers **expect clear boundaries**, welcoming AI chatbot assistance for routine tasks while favoring human support the moment an issue arises.

Each of **these friction points** plays out at a different stage of the journey, and each has distinct implications for how e-commerce sites should implement and communicate AI features.

In this article, we’ll highlight **2 high-level findings** that reflect how shoppers navigate these friction points with AI:

-   Shoppers prefer AI for low-stakes product research but favor human support for issue resolution
-   AI-generated product images reduce purchase confidence for the majority of shoppers

## Shoppers Prefer AI for Low-Stakes Research but Favor Human Support for Issue Resolution

![Stacked bar chart showing AI vs. human support preferences; shoppers prefer AI for research but favor humans for errors.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/177777/original/research-media-file-e7267db36262ab6cd2bf349803ef826d.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

While 63% of shoppers prefer AI chatbots for product research tasks like comparing features, roughly two-thirds favor human representatives when resolving order errors. (see [Quantitative Insight #AI025](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/AI025?time_period=June+2026) for an interactive version of this graph alongside key findings and actionable insights).

Online shoppers draw a **clear line in the sand** when deciding where AI fits into their customer journey, showing high willingness to adopt automated assistance for product research while rejecting it for post-purchase problems.

Our latest data reveals that a majority of shoppers prefer AI chatbots for **low-stakes information gathering**, including comparing product features (63%), checking stock availability (59%), and getting personalized recommendations (56%).

However, when order errors occur, that **preference reverses dramatically**: roughly two-thirds of respondents prefer a human representative to handle damaged or incorrect items (69%), late or missing orders (67%), and shipping or billing mistakes (66%).

This division of labor underscores that consumers view AI as a **convenient research assistant**, not a reliable proxy for high-stakes problem resolution.

Forcing shoppers into automated bot loops when an order goes wrong can introduce **severe friction**, escalating frustration at the exact moment customer retention is most vulnerable.

Retailers should therefore **align AI deployment with shopper comfort levels** by embedding chatbots natively within discovery and research workflows, while keeping live human support easily accessible.

Ensuring clear, prominent escalation pathways to human agents, or providing transparent self-service options when live support isn’t available, allows sites to leverage AI efficiency without eroding shopper trust when mistakes happen (see [Quantitative Insight #AI025](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/AI025?time_period=June%202026)).

## AI-Generated Product Images Reduce Purchase Confidence for Most Shoppers

![Bar chart showing AI image impact on buyer confidence; 60% say AI images reduce confidence, while only 18% are unaffected.](https://baymard-assets-cdn.imgix.net/research/media_files/attachments/177778/original/research-media-file-d71db1bf4c8df60508f7de504661487c.png?auto=format&dpr=2&fit=max&q=50&w=880)

While 18% of shoppers say AI-generated product images don’t affect them, 60% report that knowing an image is AI-generated reduces their purchase confidence (see [Quantitative Insight #AI027](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/AI027?time_period=June%202026) for an interactive version of this graph alongside key findings and actionable insights).

While AI tools offer retailers a cost-effective way to generate visual assets, relying on AI-generated imagery **creates a major buying hurdle** for online shoppers.

Our June 2026 survey data reveals that **60% of online shoppers** say knowing a product image is AI-generated would negatively impact their purchasing confidence.

**Specifically**, 25% report it would slightly reduce their confidence, 18% say it would majorly reduce it, and 17% state they would not feel confident enough to buy the product at all.

These figures reveal that shoppers view product imagery primarily as a **tool for verification and accuracy**, rather than mere decorative content.

Retailers should therefore **exercise caution when introducing AI-generated imagery**, prioritizing authentic photography for core product views.

When AI-generated visuals or lifestyle backgrounds are used, brands must actively take steps to counteract shopper skepticism and safeguard conversion rates (see [Quantitative Insight #AI027](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/AI027?time_period=June%202026); check out the [top features that restore buyer confidence](https://baymard.com/premium/quant-insights/AI028?time_period=June%202026).
