## Articles

[Design’s Legibility Gap](https://www.petermerholz.com/newsletter/designs-legibility-gap/) (7min) legibility is the reason why UX struggles in a lot of companies (according to Peter Merholz). Business people value predictable, measurable and only see the “pixels in the screens”. Meanwhile research and exploration often stay invisible. The job of the design lead: make the work of the UX team legible to the corporate people, while making the corporate context understandable to the design team. Yup, helping the business understand the value of open-ended, exploratory work is not an easy task!

[Design Is How It Tastes](https://superposition.jem.computer/design-is-how-it-tastes/) (12min) When designing we are often stuck into creating systems then think about the visual way. We describe things like rounded corners, tokens, and palette. And lost some of what made the “taste” of great design. Since image models can now turn sensory descriptions into working designs, Jem Gold explains that we could instead start with describing sensations, then visuals and then only then build the systems around those. I am quite curious about the approach, this sounds like putting the “vibe” into vibe design: describe the vibe, not the UI!

[Why AI is exposing design’s craft crisis](https://www.doc.cc/articles/craft-crisis) (20min) Sometimes you read something that feels like a punch in the guts, yet, you keep nodding. Here we are. This: “We told ourselves AI would level the playing field. Instead, it raised the floor. If you can’t tell the difference between functional code and broken code, between accessible design and inaccessible design, between scalable architecture and technical debt, these tools just let you fail faster.” Yup yup yup. Since the 2010s, the industry has told designers they don’t need technical skills. As a result: designers can’t join technical conversations and lose agency. The fix isn’t learning to code, it’s technical literacy: understanding APIs, data models, performance and accessibility, and evaluating AI output. That’s how designers keep strategic influence.

[The Quiet Power of Reflective Games](https://thereader.mitpress.mit.edu/the-quiet-power-of-reflective-games/) (15min) “In an industry that prizes fast-paced, time-pressured decision-making, reflective games invite players to meditate on life’s beauty, banality, and the spaces in between.” I wasn’t aware this type of game had a name, but I definitely want to play more of those!

## Useful tools & resources

[State of Ecommerce Accessibility 2026](https://davedavies.dev/state-of-ecommerce-accessibility/) 64% of product pages tested had at least one critical accessibility issue. This comes from an analysis of 59,400 product pages across many different websites. Chat makes me sad: the top 10 is again, easy fixes: contrasts, link names, heading, small targets. Folks, we can do better!

[Every Color, by Family](https://rhapsodycolor.com/color/) a nice little online tool that grouped colors in different families and lets you browse them. It then automatically calculates the contrast with either white or black text for each color in the family, and lets you also know if this color prints as is. You might also be interested in [the Figma color palette builder plugin](https://rhapsodycolor.com/palette)
