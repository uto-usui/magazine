This survey has now been running for a full decade, witnessing the rise of React, the birth of the Vite ecosystem, and the JavaScript itself being all but replaced by TypeScript; to bring up just a few of the many trends we've seen over that time.

Which brings us to 2026: in a world where AI can help you reimplement an entire framework with a different build tool, reverse engineer anything, or port your app to Rust, it's worth wondering what role JavaScript still has to play.

And it's true it's hard to be _that_ concerned about framework A vs framework B when you know coding agents can port your app from one to the other in a matter of a few hours.

But JavaScript itself still remains the gateway to the browser, and in an era where browsers are becoming more and more powerful, knowing which possibilities they offer will always have value.

So with this year's survey, let's all take one more snapshot of the State of JavaScript, and celebrate 10 years of a little programming language that grew up much, much bigger than anybody ever anticipated.
