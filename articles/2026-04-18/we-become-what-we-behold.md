## The AI medium

[Marshall McLuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_McLuhan) (July 21, 1911 — December 31, 1980) was a Canadian philosopher, media theorist, and professor at the University of Toronto’s St. Michael’s College, where he studied the effect of mass media on behavior and thought. A modern intellectual who grappled with the effects of television, mass media, and communications on society. There are many meaningful ideas and quotes attributed to him, but the quote below is particularly relevant to our current AI discourse.

Designers should become familiar with his level of thinking, media theory, and be mindful of the forces that push and pull on their creative landscape. He, along with Douglas Englebart and many others, predicted the opportunity presented by the technology and the roots of the Internet.

If you are familiar with Marshall McLuhan, I am sure you would agree that he would be losing his mind over AI, but for now, we can just assume he is rolling around in his grave. Since we are still living in the pre-wake of a singularity tsunami, let’s consider how we can build our high ground.

Let’s discuss 2 factors that are shaping how AI tools are shaping us. One, AI appeals to a user’s ego, and two, AI builds the illusion of authority, and how we can combat its hold.

### 1\. AI appeals to a user's ego

AI is a sycophant. “A_n obsequious person/entity who uses insincere flattery and servile behavior to gain advantages from influential people or their users, often referred to as a “yes-man” or “bootlicker”._

AI is not new; it has been an undercurrent of computer science from the beginning. Alan Turing is considered the godfather of AI, and he invented the Bombe machine in 1939, which helped the Allied powers beat the German Enigma to help win World War II. AI has been architected over 70 years of social science research, leveraging [habit-forming behaviors](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1591847788) for the ends of technological adoption. It is not arbitrary that AI has been successful; it has been designed to do just that and has learned well from

and others.

AI is born of humans, and whether you like it or not, they are driven by human pursuits… ego, motivated to conquer their worlds, incentivized by power, and encouraged through money. Mix that with innovation peddlers, a disruption mindset, and billions of investments, and you get a volatile environment woefully unprepared for the changes their newfound AI tools will bring. The irony, I suppose, is that Designers are, hopefully, trained not to be “yes men” but rather to ask hard questions, challenge the prevailing motivations of business over our users, and, most importantly, find the root cause of the problem, rather than just the surface reaction. AI, unfortunately, is not built to push back; it will not say… “I don’t know,” or “I think that is a bad idea,” or “what if you did this… instead,” or “I understand YOU (CEO) wants this feature, but the user research and ‘our users’ want something different.” AI is designed to serve, and in the hands of people in an organization who are looking for the least amount of pushback, it is a recipe for deep institutional implementation and, frankly, a lot of bad ideas, fast.

_Side note… It is not lost on me that my own ego is showing in this assessment as well. But self-awareness is an essential skill for a human, and a designer, and we need to recognize this unique differentiator: an empathetic, thinking, feeling human designer vs an AI, because an AI does not, at the moment, have the ability to be self-aware. It is designed, coded, and deployed with the goal of arriving at an answer._

To combat the eagerness of AI to feed your ego, apply some of these suggestions…

**AI suggestions**  
Recognize an AI’s need to please and come to it with a skeptical eye. Offset AI sycophancy by…

1.  Use prompts that explicitly demand critical evaluation, such as requesting “[devil’s advocate](https://www.google.com/search?q=devil%E2%80%99s+advocate&rlz=1C5CHFA_enUS952US952&oq=AI+prompts+that+offset+AI+sycoph&gs_lcrp=EgZjaHJvbWUqBwgBECEYoAEyBggAEEUYOTIHCAEQIRigATIHCAIQIRigATIHCAMQIRiPAjIHCAQQIRiPAtIBCTIyNjA3ajBqNKgCALACAA&sourceid=chrome&ie=UTF-8&mstk=AUtExfBXWRvqCXnLpMbw5EKUP-doUzA-8lbRnD9bSfKjnOAssiM61bQtDe8ZOSw9MBAREFwmHCcls20U4SvM8coA_c4Rv62blvGgEB58-7MReBpJ0g61PQWh02W5NwWxqFXNHayKWn-S8JOCWtpwMeiIzInP5Wbo0Ycc4SRbNjqDmGUdrsE&csui=3&ved=2ahUKEwj049a-mL6TAxXUne4BHZ_0ETsQgK4QegQIARAB)” arguments, “pros and cons” analyses, or assigning a skeptical persona.
2.  Force the AI to prioritize accuracy over agreement by instructing it to identify flaws in your logic, question your assumptions, and avoid flattering, agreeable language.
3.  Assign a Skeptical Persona**:** Assign the AI a persona like an “embittered critic,” “expert consultant,” or “hard-to-please editor” rather than a “supportive assistant”.
4.  Use Open-Ended Questions: Instead of “Is this idea good?”, ask “What are the potential drawbacks and risks of this approach?”

AI first affirms the user’s sense of self, and AI sets the stage for a second move: constructing an interface of authority that feels both natural and convincing.

### 2\. AI builds the illusion of authority

AI is seductive because of AI sycophancy, but also because it supplies answers quickly. Don’t give me wrong, this is quite convenient and useful. I am not here to tell you to stop using AI for answers. I mean, the foundation of the internet and the “Google it” ethos have generally been about accessing knowledge. The whole reason

invented the internet in the first place was to make it easier to share scientific papers using a common markup language, i.e., HTML, so I am not against the sharing of knowledge. It is nothing short of remarkable that we can quickly access the internet, academic papers, engage in dialogue around the world, and share information at the speed of light, but it has come at some cost in our new AI-infused spaces.

As a Gen X/Y, I experienced the transition to internet knowledge and had to grapple with researching online and not being able to cite internet sources, or, worse, getting my papers graded down because my research was “internet-focused.” I think about it now, and how it would be utterly ridiculous to even criticize the use of online sources in an academic paper. Furthermore, the entire education apparatus is now grappling with the impact of AI models on “cheating” in schools.

The entire process of establishing credibility in domain knowledge through truth, facts, and evolving ideas, supported by education, accreditation, and skill acquisition, is having its cages rattled by AI systems that have themselves been trained by the very education systems that now see them as a threat. The authority bestowed on institutions, educators, and SMEs (subject matter experts) is being absorbed by AI and spread thin like butter on toast. An AI appears to slather knowledge evenly, but the quality of the knowledge butter is deliberately made opaque.

The conditions that have contributed to the success of the internet have also forever modified our understanding of authenticity or information authority. Our world is fundamentally altered by the condition of access, and AI has capitalized on this availability of information. Our modern LLM models (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini 3, etc.) are all trained by the internet itself, for better or worse, and because AI is efficient and prolific, it is putting information back onto the internet, which in turn is informing its own AI outputs. Like a snake eating its own tail.

We, as designers, should work to prevent this because, as creatives, we know … “We become what we behold… we are what we eat…you reap what you sow,” et cetera, et cetera. Since AI is not going away, we should all engage in continuous training, watch for how AI can change us in not-so-positive ways, and work to make sure AI does not consume itself and us into oblivion.

**AI suggestions**  
Recognize an AI’s ability to usurp expertise and use it not for thinking but for repetitive tasks and iteration…

1\. Start away from any AI. [Use the 10–80–10 rule](https://entrylevelrebel.medium.com/steve-jobss-10-80-10-rule-is-even-more-useful-in-the-ai-era-8b003c8a9f7e). 10% away thinking, defining, establishing vision. 80% making use of AI to assist the vision. 10% away from AI critiquing, testing, and evaluating the solution.

2\. Consume your own thinking, make notes, draw more, and make it so that an AI is not good at curating your own POV before leaning on AI to help you refine your own skills.

3\. Consume those who are ringing the bell. AI moves fast, and we need more thoughtful skeptics to guide the way and hopefully keep us from changing for the worse. Follow [Kate Crawford](https://katecrawford.net/atlas) and others.

4\. Double-check sources, and make sure to include the source requirements in your AI prompts.

5\. Be more transparent. Give credit where credit is due. Source your AI models. Include prompts and avoid projecting authority when none is warranted.

### Behold AI and bend it to your will

At this point, Pandora’s box has been opened, and no amount of hand-wringing will force us back in time. But what we can do is stop being so passive; we can recognize the incursions these AI tools make into our day-to-day thinking, and we can commit to shaping how we use the tools, not the other way around. Will AI shape us? Absolutely, it already has, but how much is up to each of us, as well as a collective bunch, and I can say with a significant amount of confidence that AI is best when used to reinforce your own critical thinking, creativity, and productivity. Look out for AI when it changes any of those in negative ways.

Keep experimenting, keep making, and keep pushing forward with AI or without AI.
