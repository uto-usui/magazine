The average **overall UX** performance of the Health & Beauty sites is “mediocre”, with several sites delivering a “poor” user experience on either desktop or mobile.

Here are some of the **overarching UX issues**.

While most Health & Beauty sites supported users with curated product discovery and polished interfaces, they **struggled in areas** requiring behind-the-scenes logic or complex user flows.

Search functionality was **a weak link**, with few sites effectively [handling misspellings](https://baymard.com/guidelines/355-handling-misspellings-on-search-results-pages), [feature-based queries](https://baymard.com/guidelines/340-searching-for-products-with-specific-features), or providing relevant fallback content on [“No Results” pages](https://baymard.com/guidelines/362-designing-the-no-results-page), limiting users who relied on search to explore deeper catalog areas.

Many sites **lacked essential filters** like [“New Arrivals”](https://baymard.com/guidelines/415-new-arrivals-filters) or [“Symptom” filters](https://baymard.com/guidelines/408-symptom-filters), while others had [overlapping or duplicate filter options](https://baymard.com/guidelines/436-overlapping-or-duplicate-filter-options) or inconsistent [match counts](https://baymard.com/guidelines/467-indicating-the-number-of-matches-for-filtering-options) — undermining user control in dense product lists.

During checkout, trust-building details — such as [delivery timelines](https://baymard.com/research-articles/shipping-speed-vs-delivery-date), [order summaries](https://baymard.com/guidelines/687-order-summaries), and error [prevention](https://baymard.com/guidelines/659-restricting-field-input-characters-and-formatting) and [recovery](https://baymard.com/guidelines/719-autoscrolling-users-to-the-field-with-errors-or-an-error-summary) — were **often absent or implemented inconsistently**, undermining confidence at a critical point in the user journey.

This **mismatch** between surface-level appeal and underlying usability will leave many users unable to efficiently find, evaluate, and purchase the products they are interested in.

In summary, with **no sites** delivering a “good” overall user experience, there is plenty of room for improvement.
